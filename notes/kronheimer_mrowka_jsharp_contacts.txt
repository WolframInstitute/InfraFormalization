Direct contacts with our project — Kronheimer–Mrowka,

(in order of relevance):

1. §8.3 combinatorial counterpart `J^♭` is a TQFT-style local-evaluation
   rule on closed pre-foams: sphere-with-2-dots → 1; torus → 1;
   theta-foam(0,1,2) → 1; non-bipartite seam graph → 0; non-trivial
   monodromy on a seam circle → 0; else 0. Conjectured equal to `J^♯`
   (Conj 8.10). This *is* a spin-foam evaluation rule — exactly the
   structural shape that POLY3 (#318 spin-network/spin-foam on
   `Hypergraph V`) and J5 (#98 LQG spin-foam comparison) target. The
   axioms are short and directly portable: a Lean predicate
   `JFlat : Foam → 𝔽₂` checking the five rules is a small first
   formalisation that gives us the spin-foam category in concrete form.

2. §8.5 cube of resolutions / spectral sequence
   `E_1 = J^♯(K) ⊗ Gr` abutting to `I^♯(K)`, with
   `E_0 = ⊕_{A ⊆ {1,…,n}} C^♯(K)_A` indexed by the Boolean lattice and
   differentials `D^{A,B} = U_{B\A}` given by Steenrod ⌣₁ reduced cup
   products. This is the multiway-tree structure as a categorified
   differential: cube vertices are states, cube edges are operators,
   the abutment `→ I^♯(K)` is the "branchial → final invariant"
   projection. It is the cleanest categorical analogue I have seen of
   our `Trajectory → MultiScreener` pipeline. The abutment is on the
   same shape as the multiway-causal-graph collapse in #225.

3. §6 web-move relations prove explicit invariance / direct-sum
   behaviour of `J^♯` under bigon collapse (`dim` halves), triangle
   move (isomorphism), and square move
   (`J^♯(K) = J^♯(K') ⊕ J^♯(K'')`). §6.5 then shows
   `dim J^♯(K) = τ(K)` for any web reducible to unlinks via these
   moves. These are precisely the DEFORM1 / DEFORM2 elementary
   path/cycle deformations in our task list, with a fully worked
   example of "screener invariant under elementary moves". A natural
   target: define a Lean-side functor on hypergraphs invariant under
   our DEFORM1 moves and prove the analogues of Propositions 6.5–6.8.

4. §8.6 SU(3) variant `L^♯`: replacing SO(3) by SU(3) gives
   `dim L^♯(K; 𝔽) = τ(K)` for *any* web in 3-space — independent of
   the spatial embedding. So `L^♯` is a combinatorial invariant of the
   abstract trivalent graph alone. This is the right shape for KNOT3
   (#322 Yamada/Jones via spin-network Chern–Simons): a graph-only
   invariant defined via gauge-theoretic data on the embedding's
   complement.

5. §3.3 `d² = 0 mod 2`. The Floer differential satisfies `d² = 0 mod 2`
   because each vertex of the web contributes 4 ends to `M̄_2(α,α)`
   (the cone on `V_4`), and 4 is even mod 2. Same axiom we have
   proved over ℤ in `KnillCore.dSquared_eq_zero` (#277), with a
   similar pairing-cancellation flavour — the difference being that
   ours uses the `(-1)^i + (-1)^{i+1}` sign-pair on the simplicial
   cochain complex while theirs uses a 4-fold codim-2 vertex-bubbling
   cancellation in the instanton moduli. Different setting, same
   axiom; their argument is a useful comparison case for future
   generalisations of our `d² = 0` to richer chain complexes.

6. §2.6 index formula
   `d(E,A) = 8κ − 3(1 − b¹(X) + b⁺(X)) + χ(Σ) + ½ Σ·Σ − ½|τ|`
   mixes gauge-theoretic terms (`κ` = action, `b⁺`) with purely
   combinatorial ones (`χ(Σ)` Euler characteristic of the foam, `|τ|`
   tetrahedral-point count, `Σ·Σ` self-intersection). Same flavour as
   discrete Gauss-Bonnet — the AT2 Forman-defect /
   Knill-Euler-Poincaré identity in our IGE1 / KN8 modules.

7. §8.4 gradings via bipartiteness — `J(Y,K;μ)` admits a ℤ/d grading
   iff the abstract graph is bipartite (else only ℤ/2). The action `κ`
   lies in `N/8 mod (1/4)ℤ` where `N` is the count of "Type II"
   vertices defined by an `η(e) ∈ {0,1}` pairing data on edges. Graph
   bipartiteness is a basic combinatorial property our hypergraph
   predicates already track; the obstruction-class structure
   (`w_2`-pulled-back) is something we would need for a serious Lean
   port.

8. §7 sutured-manifold non-vanishing chain. The proof that
   `J^♯(K) ≠ 0 ↔ no embedded bridge` runs through: excise vertices →
   connected sum with `S¹×S²` summands → meridional sutured manifold
   `(M, γ)` → tautness → Gabai's hierarchy theorem. Sutured-manifold
   theory is not in Mathlib and would be a major formalisation
   enterprise of its own; not on our path.
