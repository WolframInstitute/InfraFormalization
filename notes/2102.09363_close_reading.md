## 2. Role of the hypergraph in the CCZ4 solver

§2-§3 present the Z4 and CCZ4 formulations of the Einstein equations (eqs. 1-121). §4 (lines 1635-2527) solves them on the hypergraph as an unstructured mesh: fourth-order Runge-Kutta in time (eqs. 122-127), fourth-order finite differences (eqs. 128-132), interpolation (eqs. 133-134), Kreiss-Oliger dissipation (eqs. 135-137), a graph version of Berger-Colella adaptive mesh refinement (eqs. 138-158, from line 1915) and WENO reconstruction (eqs. 159-170, from line 2332). The hypergraph supplies the grid points, an inner product on graph geodesics (lines 1795-1813), and the partition used for refinement (eq. 151, lines 2168-2177). G3 states that this inner product "will satisfy the requisite axioms of linearity and conjugate symmetry if and only if the hypergraph has a reasonable interpretation as a continuous manifold in the continuum limit" (lines 1809-1811). The CCZ4 equations are not derived from hypergraph rewriting in §2-§5.

In §5 the captions of the hypergraph figures state that "the vertices have been assigned spatial coordinates according to the profile of the Schwarzschild conformal factor ψ" (Figure 11, lines 2722-2726; Figure 12, lines 2730-2734). The §6 initial data are built differently, by a Poisson point process in a given (M, g) (lines 6148-6176).

## 4. The Benincasa-Dowker chain (lines 5940-6152)

The steps below follow G3's order.

Step (A), eq. 379 (lines 5990-6001): the d = 4 causal-layer operator B⁽⁴⁾φ(e) = (1/l²)[−(4/√6)φ(e) + (4/√6)(Σ_{L₀}φ − 9Σ_{L₁}φ + 16Σ_{L₂}φ − 8Σ_{L₃}φ)], from Benincasa-Dowker [13].

Step (B), eqs. 380-383 (lines 6011-6071): an intermediate scale l_k and a smoothed expectation over J⁻(x). Lines 6063-6067: "In the continuum limit ρc → ∞, the contributions to this integral from within the Riemann normal neighborhood W1 of event x will be given, up to first-order, by the continuum d'Alembertian □φ(x), and up to second-order by the spacetime Ricci curvature scalar R(x) at event x", giving eq. 383. This is the Benincasa-Dowker result, which concerns the expectation over Poisson sprinklings into a given Lorentzian manifold (M, g).

Step (C), lines 6077-6084: "Thus, starting from the assumption that the coupling constants satisfy ε(u, v) = 1 for all pairs of vertices u and v, and replacing the assumption of weak ergodicity of the causal graph dynamics with the much stronger assumption of uniform Poisson distribution of the rewrite events, our construction of the discrete Einstein-Hilbert action for Wolfram model systems reduces to the general d-dimensional form of the Benincasa-Dowker action on causal sets". The Poisson distribution of rewrite events is an assumption of this step. G3 gives no proof that a Wolfram model rule produces Poisson-distributed rewrite events. The weak ergodicity hypothesis is referred to in §1 (lines 144-148 and 271-275) as known from earlier work ("which have previously been shown to yield extremization of the discrete Einstein-Hilbert action", line 275) without a citation at those lines.

Step (D), eqs. 384-387 (lines 6086-6124): lines 6110-6120 state that, "as proposed by Sorkin, Benincasa and Dowker, and demonstrated numerically by Cunningham[108], so long as one assumes zero surface terms, and moreover so long as the contributions to the integral from further down the light cone (i.e. from the region outside the Riemann normal neighborhood W₁) vanish in the limit", the expectation of S⁽⁴⁾ converges to S_EH(g). Within the causal-set literature this limit uses three assumptions: (i) zero surface terms, (ii) vanishing contributions from outside W₁, with numerical support from Cunningham, and (iii) Poisson sprinkling.

Step (E), lines 6142-6152: "Now that we have the ability to extract arbitrary curvature information from the combinatorial structure of a given hypergraph and/or causal graph, and we have techniques for determining when a given Wolfram model evolution rule satisfies the Einstein field equations (through extremization of the Einstein-Hilbert action) in the continuum limit, we can proceed to define the initial data for our relativistic Cauchy problem by constructing a hypergraph H that is guaranteed to be faithfully embeddable into a given Lorentzian manifold (M, g), with embedding map Φ, by means of a Poisson point process." The initial hypergraph is a Poisson sprinkling into the Brill-Lindquist spatial slice (eqs. 389-390, line 6179). The rule of line 6202 is then run on this initial hypergraph.

Status of each ingredient:

- Proved in the causal-set literature: the d = 2 and d = 4 operator expectations converge to □ − ½R for Poisson sprinklings into a fixed (M, g) (Benincasa-Dowker 2010, [13]; Dowker-Glaser 2013, [105]).
- Conjectured in the causal-set literature: (i) the statement that a causal set faithfully embeds into two Lorentzian manifolds only if they are approximately isometric, which G3 describes as "conjectured (although not yet proved)" (lines 4974-4982, citing [95]); (ii) vanishing of surface terms; (iii) vanishing of contributions from outside W₁.
- Numerically supported in the causal-set literature: convergence of the BD action to the Einstein-Hilbert action on Poisson sprinklings (Cunningham 2018, [108]).
- Stated in G3 without proof: (i) Wolfram model rules satisfying weak ergodicity and asymptotic dimension preservation produce Poisson-distributed rewrite events; (ii) the rule of line 6202 satisfies these conditions.

References cited for the chain:

- [13] Benincasa and Dowker, "Scalar Curvature of a Causal Set", PRL 104, 181301 (2010) (line 6632): the d = 4 causal-set d'Alembertian and the convergence of its Poisson expectation to □ − ½R.
- [105] Dowker and Glaser, CQG 30, 195016 (2013) (line 7127): causal-set d'Alembertians in other dimensions, for Poisson sprinklings.
- [106] Sorkin (2007) (line 7131) and [107] Henson (2009) (line 7135): the d = 2 operator and a review.
- [108] Cunningham, PhD thesis (2018) (line 7139): numerical evidence that the BD action converges to the Einstein-Hilbert action on sprinklings.
- [94] Bombelli and Noldus (2004) (line 7074) and [95] Bombelli, Noldus and Tafoya (2012) (line 7078): the approximate-isometry conjecture.

These references concern Poisson sprinklings of given Lorentzian manifolds. None of them states a result about hypergraph rewriting systems.

## 5. The rule of line 6202

The rule {{x,y},{y,z},{z,w},{w,v}} → {{y,u},{u,v},{w,x},{x,u}} is named in the text at lines 6199-6202 and in the captions of Figures 73-77 and 79-83. G3 uses it for the Schwarzschild and Kerr head-on collisions at 100 to 1600 vertices.

G3 gives no proof for this rule. Specifically G3 does not show that the rule is confluent (lines 5036-5038 call confluence "generally necessary (though not sufficient) to guarantee causal invariance"), satisfies weak ergodicity, satisfies asymptotic dimension preservation, or produces Poisson-distributed rewrite events. G3 gives no map from evolutions of the rule to a Lorentzian manifold other than the Poisson-sprinkled initial data of eqs. 389-390.

G3 describes the rule as one "that provably satisfies the Einstein field equations in the continuum limit" (abstract, line 51; §1, lines 182 and 271), and §7 states that the rules "were known a priori to satisfy the Einstein field equations in the continuum limit" (line 6492). §1 (lines 271-275) states that such rules are located through weak ergodicity and asymptotic dimension preservation. G1 (arXiv:2004.14810, cited as [8]) contains the argument for that link; its steps are listed in [`2004.14810_close_reading.md`](2004.14810_close_reading.md) §3.3.

The repository's results on this rule: edge-count conservation (PROVED, `Refutations.gorardNamedRule_applications_preserve_size`); antichain bound 4·|A| ≤ N and cone bound 5^t for every update order (PROVED, `CausalHistory.gorardNamedRule_antichain_card_le`, `CausalHistory.gorardNamedRule_ball_card_le`); cone-growth exponents between 0.77 and 1.26 on cycles, paths and 3D grids, depending on the update order (MEASURED, [`empirical_refutation.md`](empirical_refutation.md) §4); linear cone growth OPEN (`CausalHistory.GorardNamedRuleConeGrowthLinear`).

## 6. Results reported in §5 and §6

§5.1-§5.5 report the CCZ4 solver on the hypergraph mesh for static Schwarzschild, rotating Kerr, maximally extended Schwarzschild, a Schwarzschild head-on collision and a Kerr head-on collision. Tables 1-5 give L1, L2 and L∞ norms of the Hamiltonian constraint with "approximately fourth-order convergence". These tables test the PDE solver.

§6 (lines 6196-6463, Tables 6 and 7) compares the CCZ4 solution with the evolution under the rule for the Schwarzschild and Kerr head-on collisions. G3 reports:

- Figures 73-77 and 79-83 show the two sets of hypergraphs side by side, "colored using the local curvature in the Schwarzschild conformal factor ψ" (Figure 73 caption, lines 6251-6256), where ψ is the analytic expression of eq. 391.
- Tables 6 and 7 give Hamiltonian-constraint norms of the rule's evolution at t = 24M with "approximately second-order convergence".
- Figures 78 and 84 show the l = 2, m = 0 mode of rΨ4 at t = 12M at 200, 400 and 800 vertices.

The comparison starts from initial data sampled from the target geometry by the Poisson construction of lines 6148-6176. G3 does not state the link cut-off distance ("a certain cut-off distance", line 6176), the orientation of the hyperedges, the update order, the number of events per unit of t, or the procedures that compute ψ, rΨ4 and the Hamiltonian constraint from a hypergraph. G3 does not report a run of the rule from initial data that is not sampled from a black-hole geometry.

The statement that the two sets of discrete spacetimes "converge to the same sets of Lorentzian manifolds in the continuum limit" (lines 186 and 277-279) rests on the constraint-norm scaling and the rΨ4 plots. G3 does not state a notion of convergence of hypergraphs to Lorentzian manifolds and does not compare metric components.

The norms of eqs. 180-181 (lines 2671-2687) satisfy L∞ ≤ L1 ≤ L2 for every H, and 28 of the 35 rows of Tables 1-7 violate this ordering, including all five rows of Table 6 (MEASURED, [`results/phase2_continuum/convergence_tables.md`](../results/phase2_continuum/convergence_tables.md)). The printed orders are log₂ of the norm ratio per doubling of the vertex count, and three of them differ from the tabulated norms by more than 0.01. G3 states no relation between the vertex count and the grid spacing. At 1600 vertices the L1 norm of the rule's Schwarzschild head-on run (Table 6, line 6231) is 6.3 × 10³ times the CCZ4 value for the same problem (Table 4, line 4480).

The rΨ4 curves of the rule in Figures 78 and 84 follow the CCZ4 curves of the same problem in Figures 39 and 49, with Pearson r = 0.998 (Schwarzschild) and 0.995 (Kerr) between digitised curves, against r = 0.61-0.62 between the Schwarzschild and Kerr curves of either method. The rΨ4 curve of Gravitas II Figure 54 (arXiv:2401.14209, ADM, masses M at separation 2M, 10,000 vertices) equals the 800-vertex curve of Figure 39 to about two pixels (r = 1.000 and 0.9999 by two independent digitisations), and r = 0.05-0.08 with the time axis stretched by 2, the factor that relates the two systems (MEASURED, [`results/phase2_continuum/rpsi4_digitisation.md`](../results/phase2_continuum/rpsi4_digitisation.md)).

## 7. Summary

- §1-§5 present a CCZ4 solver on an unstructured hypergraph mesh with adaptive refinement and WENO reconstruction, tested on five black-hole problems with fourth-order convergence of the Hamiltonian constraint (Tables 1-5).
- G3 gives no proof that the rule of line 6202 satisfies the Einstein field equations, weak ergodicity, asymptotic dimension preservation or the Poisson assumption.
- The BD chain assumes Poisson-distributed rewrite events (line 6080), zero surface terms and vanishing contributions from outside W₁ (lines 6110-6120).
- The §6 comparison starts from a hypergraph sampled by a Poisson process from the Brill-Lindquist geometry (lines 6148-6179), and G3 does not state the cut-off distance, the update order or the number of events per unit of t.
- Eqs. 342 and 344 differ from Gray (1973) and Ollivier (2009) (S1 in [`severity_taxonomy.md`](severity_taxonomy.md)).

## Appendix A. Quotations and assumptions for a Lean statement

1. Lines 271-275 (§1): "by selecting a hypergraph transformation rule that provably satisfies the Einstein field equations in the continuum limit, which can in turn be done by locating rules which satisfy weak ergodicity and asymptotic dimension preservation (which have previously been shown to yield extremization of the discrete Einstein-Hilbert action)." No citation is given at these lines.
2. Lines 4974-4982 (§6): "it has been conjectured (although not yet proved) that a single causal graph whose set of rewrite events is C can be faithfully embedded (assuming a fixed density ρc) into a pair of distinct Lorentzian manifolds ... if and only if the manifolds ... are themselves approximately isometric[95]."
3. Lines 6077-6084 (§6): "starting from the assumption that the coupling constants satisfy ε(u, v) = 1 for all pairs of vertices u and v, and replacing the assumption of weak ergodicity of the causal graph dynamics with the much stronger assumption of uniform Poisson distribution of the rewrite events, our construction of the discrete Einstein-Hilbert action for Wolfram model systems reduces to the general d-dimensional form of the Benincasa-Dowker action on causal sets".
4. Lines 6110-6120 (§6): "as proposed by Sorkin, Benincasa and Dowker, and demonstrated numerically by Cunningham[108], so long as one assumes zero surface terms, and moreover so long as the contributions to the integral from further down the light cone (i.e. from the region outside the Riemann normal neighborhood W1) vanish in the limit, then the expectation value of the discrete action S(4) will converge to the continuum Einstein-Hilbert action SEH(g) on the manifold (M, g) in the continuum limit ρc → ∞".
5. Lines 6148-6152 (§6): "we can proceed to define the initial data for our relativistic Cauchy problem by constructing a hypergraph H that is guaranteed to be faithfully embeddable into a given Lorentzian manifold (M, g), with embedding map Φ, by means of a Poisson point process."

Assumptions a Lean statement of the chain would carry:

- A1 (Poisson). The vertices of Φ(H) ⊂ (M, g) are a Poisson point process of density ρc (eqs. 389-390).
- A2 (Cut-off). Two vertices are linked iff their images lie within a fixed cut-off distance (lines 6174-6176). G3 does not state its value.
- A3 (Couplings). ε(u, v) = 1 for all vertex pairs (line 6078).
- A4 (Zero surface terms). Line 6112.
- A5 (Vanishing contributions from outside W₁). Lines 6114-6116.
- A6 (Intermediate scale). l_k ≫ (l² L)^(1/3) (eq. 388, TeX source `tex_2102/NumericalRelativity.tex:2926`).
- A7 (Riemann normal neighbourhood W₁). Line 6065.
- A8 (Approximate isometry). The Bombelli-Noldus conjecture (lines 4974-4982).
- A9 (Rule to Poisson). A rule with weak ergodicity and asymptotic dimension preservation produces rewrite events satisfying A1. G3 states this as an assumption (line 6080) and gives no proof.
- A10 (Rule in class). The rule of line 6202 satisfies the hypotheses of A9. G3 gives no argument for it.

Under A1-A7, Benincasa-Dowker (2010) gives eq. 383 for the expectation. Eq. 387 further needs A4, A5 and a concentration statement for the expectation, which in the causal-set literature has numerical support (Cunningham). A9 and A10 are the steps specific to the Wolfram model; both are OPEN.
