## 2. Is CCZ4 being solved on the hypergraph, or derived from it?

Unambiguous from §2–§4: **CCZ4 is solved on the hypergraph as an unstructured mesh**. The equations are the standard Bona–Palenzuela–Alic–Rezzolla–Bona-Casas CCZ4 PDEs (eqs. 1–121 verbatim from the textbook literature). §4 (lines 1635–2527) applies explicit 4th-order Runge-Kutta in time (eqs. 125–127), 4th-order centered finite differences in space (eqs. 128–132), Kreiss–Oliger dissipation (eqs. 135–137), Berger–Colella AMR generalized to hypergraphs (eqs. 138–150), and WENO reconstruction (eqs. 159–170). The hypergraph supplies only: (i) vertex positions used as grid points; (ii) an inner product from graph geodesics (lines 1791–1815) so directional derivatives make sense; (iii) refinement/coarsening criteria from curvature invariants (eqs. 151, 152). Nothing about CCZ4 is *derived* from hypergraph rewriting; CCZ4 is a pre-existing PDE system imported wholesale.

The initial data is set on a hypergraph but the hypergraph in §5 is **generated by sprinkling vertices according to an analytic Schwarzschild/Kerr conformal factor** (lines 2555–2610, 2722–2734), not by any rewriting rule. See also §6 line 6152: "we construct the elements of Φ(H) using a Poisson distribution" into a pre-given (M,g).

## 4. The Benincasa–Dowker chain — exact logical flow

Reproduced verbatim from §6, lines 5980–6140. I preserve Gorard's own logical order, then annotate.

**Step (A)** — *d=4 BD operator* (eq. 379):
> "B⁽⁴⁾ φ(e) = (1/l²)[(−4/√6)φ(e) + (4/√6){Σ_{L₀}φ − 9Σ_{L₁}φ + 16Σ_{L₂}φ − 8Σ_{L₃}φ}]"

— causal-layer ansatz taken verbatim from Benincasa–Dowker [13].

**Step (B)** — *smoothed d'Alembertian*, eq. 380. Introduces an intermediate scale lk ≥ l and writes an expectation ⟨B̂ₖ⁽ᵈ⁾φ(x)⟩ as an integral over J⁻(x) weighted by exp(−Vol I[y,x]·lₖ⁻ᵈ). Gorard: "In the continuum limit ρc → ∞, the contributions to this integral from within the Riemann normal neighborhood W₁ of event x will be given, up to first-order, by the continuum d'Alembertian φ(x), and up to second-order by the spacetime Ricci curvature scalar R(x) at event x" (line 6063-6067), yielding eq. 383:
> "lim_{ρc→∞} (1/√ρc)⟨B̂ₖ⁽ᵈ⁾φ(x)⟩|_{W₁} = □φ(x) − (1/2) R(x) φ(x)"

This is the Benincasa–Dowker theorem — but it is a theorem **about the expectation over Poisson sprinklings into a prescribed Lorentzian manifold (M,g)**. It is not a theorem about arbitrary causal graphs, let alone causal graphs produced by a rewriting rule.

**Step (C)** — *the unproved step at line 6078–6084* (verbatim):
> "Thus, starting from the assumption that the coupling constants satisfy ε(u,v) = 1 for all pairs of vertices u and v, and **replacing the assumption of weak ergodicity of the causal graph dynamics with the much stronger assumption of uniform Poisson distribution of the rewrite events**, our construction of the discrete Einstein-Hilbert action for Wolfram model systems reduces to the general d-dimensional form of the Benincasa-Dowker action on causal sets"

This is the whole derivation. The phrase "replacing ... with the much stronger assumption" is a concession that the required hypothesis (uniform Poisson) is **not** delivered by Wolfram-model dynamics; Gorard *imports it*. The reference to "weak ergodicity" is a pointer to unpublished / earlier Gorard work (no internal citation given here; compare the §1 introduction wording at lines 150–154 and 272–275: "previously been shown to yield extremization of the discrete Einstein-Hilbert action" — again no citation of a specific theorem).

**Step (D)** — *the BD action and continuum limit* (eqs. 384–387). Under the Poisson assumption just imported, Gorard cites [Sorkin, Benincasa, Dowker; Cunningham 2018 [108] numerically]:
> "so long as one assumes zero surface terms, and moreover so long as the contributions to the integral from further down the light cone (i.e. from the region outside the Riemann normal neighborhood W₁) vanish in the limit, then the expectation value of the discrete action S⁽⁴⁾ will converge to the continuum Einstein-Hilbert action S_EH(g) on the manifold (M,g) in the continuum limit ρc → ∞"

— i.e. **even within the causal-set programme**, the continuum limit of the BD action requires (i) zero surface terms (unproven in general), (ii) vanishing non-local contributions from outside the Riemann normal neighbourhood (unproven in general; numerically supported in special cases by Cunningham), on top of (iii) the Poisson assumption.

**Step (E)** — *closure of the loop* (lines 6142–6152):
> "Now that we have the ability to extract arbitrary curvature information from the combinatorial structure of a given hypergraph and/or causal graph, and we have techniques for determining when a given Wolfram model evolution rule satisfies the Einstein field equations (through extremization of the Einstein-Hilbert action) in the continuum limit, **we can proceed to define the initial data for our relativistic Cauchy problem by constructing a hypergraph H that is guaranteed to be faithfully embeddable into a given Lorentzian manifold (M,g), with embedding map Φ, by means of a Poisson point process.**"

This is the decisive sentence. The hypergraph H used in the numerical comparison is literally *constructed by Poisson-sprinkling into the known Schwarzschild / Kerr / Brill–Lindquist spacetime* (eqs. 389–390). It is not produced by the substitution rule. The substitution rule is then run *on top of* an initial data hypergraph which is by construction a valid BD causal set for the target manifold.

**Classification of the chain (a/b/c/d):**

- (a) *Proven in causal-set literature*: BD d=2, d=4 operator expectation → □−R/2 under Poisson sprinkling into fixed (M,g), up to proofs in Benincasa–Dowker 2010 (PRL 104, 181301). Technical extensions: Dowker–Glaser 2013.
- (b) *Conjectured in causal-set literature*: (i) the Bombelli–Noldus "Hauptvermutung" that a causal set faithfully embeds into at most one (M,g) up to approximate isometry (explicitly flagged in Gorard's own text at line 4976 "conjectured although not yet proved"); (ii) vanishing of surface terms; (iii) vanishing of non-local light-cone contributions outside W₁.
- (c) *Numerically supported in causal-set literature*: Cunningham 2018 PhD thesis [108] — numerical evidence for BD action → EH action on Poisson sprinklings.
- (d) *Gorard's own assumption, imported without proof*: (i) Wolfram-model rules satisfying "weak ergodicity + asymptotic dimension preservation" produce Poisson-distributed causal-graph vertices; (ii) the specific rule `{{x,y},{y,z},{z,w},{w,v}} → {{y,u},{u,v},{w,x},{x,u}}` satisfies those conditions.

Cited references and what they actually prove:
- **[13] Benincasa–Dowker (PRL 104, 181301, 2010)**: Defines the d=4 causal-set d'Alembertian and proves that its Poisson expectation, with intermediate smoothing scale, converges to □ − R/2 in the continuum limit — for Poisson sprinklings. Gorard uses this *correctly*, but only applies to the Poisson-sprinkled hypergraphs, not to rule-generated ones.
- **[105] Dowker–Glaser (CQG 30, 195016, 2013)**: Generalizes the BD operator to other dimensions. Same framework (Poisson sprinkling).
- **[106] Sorkin (2007) / [107] Henson (2009)**: d=2 case and review.
- **[108] Cunningham (PhD thesis 2018)**: Numerical evidence that the BD action converges to the EH action on sprinklings.
- **[94] Bombelli–Noldus (2004) / [95] Bombelli–Noldus–Tafoya (2012)**: The conjectural approximate isometry result (Hauptvermutung / moduli-space work) — explicitly cited by Gorard as "conjectured (although not yet proved)" at line 4976.

None of [13],[105],[106],[107],[108] prove anything about abstract hypergraph rewriting systems. They all concern Poisson sprinklings of given Lorentzian manifolds.

## 5. The "pure Wolfram model rule that provably satisfies EFE" — what is it, and what is the proof?

**The rule.** `{{x, y}, {y, z}, {z, w}, {w, v}} → {{y, u}, {u, v}, {w, x}, {x, u}}` (first appearance line 6202; reused throughout §6 for every comparison: Schwarzschild head-on, Kerr head-on, at resolutions 200 / 400 / 800 / 1600).

**The proof given in the paper for this rule.** None. The rule is named once in a parenthetical inside Figure 73's caption (line 6202) and then referenced identically in Figs. 74–77, 79–83. There is:
- no demonstration that this rule is confluent (despite confluence being declared necessary earlier, eq. 327);
- no demonstration that this rule satisfies weak ergodicity;
- no demonstration that this rule satisfies asymptotic dimension preservation;
- no exhibition of the rule's causal graph's Poisson statistics;
- no mapping from iterates of the rule onto a target Lorentzian manifold other than *the Poisson-sprinkled initial data from §6 eq. 389–390*.

**The proof sketch Gorard implies (reconstructed).** Claims in §1 (lines 146–154, 272–275) and §6 (lines 6142–6148) amount to: "rules satisfying weak ergodicity + asymptotic dimension preservation have previously been shown to yield extremization of the discrete Einstein-Hilbert action." No internal citation or forward citation is given at those lines; the relevant earlier Gorard preprints (e.g. arXiv:2004.14810) are indeed cited elsewhere ([8]) but do not contain a rigorous theorem of that form — and even if they did, no verification is given that the specific rule above is in that class.

**Assessment.** The phrase "provably satisfies the Einstein field equations in the continuum limit" (abstract, line 51; §1, lines 182, 271; §7, line 6491) is **not supported** in §6. The actual content is: (i) build initial data by Poisson-sprinkling into a known BH solution; (ii) run the rewriting rule forward; (iii) observe numerically that the resulting hypergraph tracks the PDE solver to 2nd-order accuracy. Steps (i)–(iii) are consistent with the rule doing *nothing gravitationally coherent* as long as the *initial data* is BD-compatible and the rule preserves the local combinatorial structure.

## 6. What §7 and §5 actually show

§5.1–§5.5 show 4th-order convergence of the CCZ4-on-hypergraph-mesh solver against Schwarzschild, Kerr, maximally-extended Schwarzschild, Schwarzschild head-on merger, and Kerr head-on merger. Tables 1–5 tabulate Hamiltonian-constraint L¹, L², L∞ convergence rates. This is a legitimate validation of the PDE solver. Gravitational waves extracted via rΨ₄ on a coordinate sphere (Figs. e.g. 78, 84) live on the CCZ4 continuum metric interpolated onto the hypergraph, not on the rule.

§6 (lines 6196–6463, Tables 6 and 7) numerically compares the CCZ4-on-mesh solution to the Wolfram-model-rule evolution for head-on Schwarzschild and Kerr mergers. What is actually reported:
- Hypergraphs appear similar side-by-side (Figures 73–77, 79–83) when colored by the Schwarzschild/Boyer-Lindquist conformal factor ψ.
- The pure-Wolfram-model evolution exhibits ≈ 2nd-order convergence of the Hamiltonian-constraint L²-norm (Tables 6, 7).
- The l=2, m=0 mode of rΨ₄ at t=12M is shown to converge (Figs. 78, 84) under mesh refinement.

What this numerically *proves*:
- That the pure-Wolfram-model evolution, *starting from a Poisson-sprinkled initial hypergraph built from the known Schwarzschild/Kerr metric*, stays close (in the Hamiltonian-constraint L² sense, read off via Ollivier–Ricci discrete curvature, evaluated on a specific gauge foliation) to the CCZ4 evolution of the same analytic spacetime.

What it does *not* prove:
- That "same spacetime" holds pointwise on the metric components; the comparison is via constraint-norm convergence on a chosen slicing and Ollivier-Ricci-derived curvature quantities. Diffeomorphism equivalence is not demonstrated.
- That the rule would produce a BH-like hypergraph from *generic* initial data (e.g. flat or non-BH sprinkling).
- That the rule by itself — without BD-Poisson initial data — induces any Lorentzian geometry.
- That the map from discrete Ollivier-Ricci curvature to continuum Ricci is more than leading-order correct (the comparison uses ψ-conformal-factor coloring, which is *already* knowledge of the continuum answer).

The "two discrete spacetimes converge to the same Lorentzian manifold" claim (line 281) is supported only in the sense "constraint norms have the same scaling and rΨ₄ extracts agree to plotting precision"; it is not a diffeomorphism-equivalence statement and certainly not a proof that the *rule* encodes GR.

## 7. Verdict (~200 words)

This paper is a numerical general relativity paper with Wolfram-model garnishing. §1–§5 present a legitimate, competent implementation of a CCZ4/BSSN solver on an unstructured (hypergraph) mesh with Berger–Colella AMR and WENO reconstruction, validated on standard BH test problems to 4th-order convergence. That is real work.

The Wolfram-model claim — that there exists "a hypergraph substitution rule that provably satisfies the Einstein field equations in the continuum limit" — is not established in this paper. The sole rule actually used, `{{x,y},{y,z},{z,w},{w,v}} → {{y,u},{u,v},{w,x},{x,u}}`, is named once and never shown to satisfy the hypotheses (weak ergodicity, asymptotic dimension preservation) that are themselves only heuristically linked to Poisson-distributed rewrite events. The Benincasa–Dowker chain is invoked correctly but applied only after **the Poisson distribution has been imported as an assumption** (line 6080), and after **the hypergraph has been built by Poisson-sprinkling into the target Schwarzschild / Kerr manifold** (line 6152, eq. 389). The numerical agreement in §6 is therefore between *two different discretizations of the same pre-specified continuum solution*, not evidence that the rule derives GR from first principles. The "derivation" is circular: GR is assumed to obtain the initial data, then recovered.

## Appendix A — Precise quotes for Lean formalization

1. **Line 272–275 (§1):** "by locating rules which satisfy weak ergodicity and asymptotic dimension preservation (which have previously been shown to yield extremization of the discrete Einstein-Hilbert action)." — *No internal citation. No theorem statement given anywhere in the paper.*

2. **Line 4976–4980 (§6):** "it has been conjectured (although not yet proved) that a single causal graph whose set of rewrite events is C can be faithfully embedded (assuming a fixed density ρc) into a pair of distinct Lorentzian manifolds ... if and only if the manifolds ... are themselves approximately isometric[95]." — *Hauptvermutung, unproven, flagged as such by Gorard himself.*

3. **Line 6078–6084 (§6):** "starting from the assumption that the coupling constants satisfy ε(u,v) = 1 for all pairs of vertices u and v, and replacing the assumption of weak ergodicity of the causal graph dynamics with the much stronger assumption of uniform Poisson distribution of the rewrite events, our construction of the discrete Einstein-Hilbert action for Wolfram model systems reduces to the general d-dimensional form of the Benincasa-Dowker action on causal sets."

4. **Line 6110–6120 (§6):** "as proposed by Sorkin, Benincasa and Dowker, and demonstrated numerically by Cunningham[108], so long as one assumes zero surface terms, and moreover so long as the contributions to the integral from further down the light cone (i.e. from the region outside the Riemann normal neighborhood W₁) vanish in the limit, then the expectation value of the discrete action S⁽⁴⁾ will converge to the continuum Einstein-Hilbert action S_EH(g) on the manifold (M,g) in the continuum limit."

5. **Line 6148–6152 (§6):** "we can proceed to define the initial data for our relativistic Cauchy problem by constructing a hypergraph H that is guaranteed to be faithfully embeddable into a given Lorentzian manifold (M,g), with embedding map Φ, by means of a Poisson point process."

Precise assumptions needed, for Lean:
- **A1 (Poisson).** Vertices of H in Φ(H) ⊂ (M,g) are a Poisson point process with density ρc.
- **A2 (Hyperedge from cut-off).** Two vertices are linked iff their Φ-images are within a fixed cut-off (line 6174).
- **A3 (Couplings).** ε(u,v) = 1 for all vertex pairs (line 6078).
- **A4 (Zero surface terms).** Line 6112.
- **A5 (Vanishing light-cone tail).** Line 6114.
- **A6 (Intermediate scale).** l ≪ lk ≪ L^(1/3) (eq. 388).
- **A7 (Riemann normal neighborhood W₁).** Line 6065.
- **A8 (Hauptvermutung).** Bombelli–Noldus approximate-isometry conjecture (unproven; line 4976).
- **A9 (Rule → Poisson).** Claimed but not proven: a rule with weak ergodicity + asymptotic dimension preservation produces vertex distributions satisfying A1. *This is the load-bearing unproven step.*
- **A10 (Specific rule in class).** That `{{x,y},{y,z},{z,w},{w,v}} → {{y,u},{u,v},{w,x},{x,u}}` satisfies the conditions in A9. *Not demonstrated anywhere in the paper.*

Under A1–A7, Benincasa–Dowker 2010 gives eq. 383 as a theorem (for the expectation). Eq. 387 further requires A4, A5, A8 and some form of "concentration of the expectation," which in the causal-set literature is partly numerical (Cunningham). Under A9 + A10 the chain closes — but A9 and A10 are precisely the Wolfram-model-specific steps that are *not* proved in this paper (or, to our knowledge, anywhere).
