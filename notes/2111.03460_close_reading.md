## 2. Claims

### C-1 Hypergraph definition (lines 161-167)

"A Wolfram model hypergraph ... is a finite collection of hyperedges (ordered): E ⊂ P(V) \ {∅}, ... and E is an ordered collection of nodes." Elements of P(V) are unordered sets without repeated elements. The initial state of Figure 5 (line 231) is {{0,0},{0,0}}, two equal hyperedges each with a repeated vertex, which is not a subset of P(V) \ {∅}. CONTRADICTED by the paper's own example. The same definition appears in G1 Def 1 (`2004.14810_close_reading.md` §2.1) and G5 Def 9 (eq. 6).

### C-2 Confluence and causal invariance (lines 258-264)

> "The notion of confluence in abstract rewriting theory is deeply related to (and, indeed, is a necessary but not sufficient condition for) the criterion of causal invariance ... For the particular case of a terminating (strongly normalizing) rewriting system, causal invariance therefore implies that all evolution paths yield the same eventual state."

CONTRADICTED. MEASURED (`qm_confluence_causal_invariance.py`, system E1): the terminating system A → B, A → C (as {a} → {a,a}, {a} → {a,a,a}) is causal invariant under the definition of line 261 (both complete histories have one event) and has two distinct terminal states. The statement that confluence is not sufficient agrees with systems E2 and E3 of the same script. G5 (lines 499-503) states the opposite, that for terminating systems convergence of branch pairs is sufficient (`STATUS_OF_GORARD_CLAIMS.md` Claim 6).

### C-3 Branchial graph definition (lines 268-270)

"states are connected by directed edges if and only if they share a common ancestor in the evolution graph". In a multiway system with one initial state every pair of states shares the initial state as an ancestor, so this graph is complete on every slice. G5 (lines 1508-1510) joins states that share an immediate predecessor, and so does HGEvolve. READ.

### C-4 Bijective coalgebra and rulial space (lines 293-302, 347-350)

The text of G6 lines 112-169 is repeated: the labelled rewriting system is "simply a bijective function from A to a subset of the power set" (line 294), CONTRADICTED as in G6 C-2; the rulial space acquires a selective adhesive structure, STATED; its monoidal structure is CITED to [Gorard2020c].

### C-5 Type constructors for string multiway systems (lines 389-512)

G7 gives inference rules for a monoid type S, a dependent type M of multiway states, an inclusion i : M ↪ S with inverse, a tripartition map and a conditional rewriting rule. Line 456: "any term s : S is, by construction, a disjoint union of the generators" (eq. at line 458, a coproduct ∐ s_{k_l}). A word of a monoid is a product (concatenation) of generators, not a coproduct. Line 446 defines the inverse of i on all of S, and i is an inclusion that is not surjective (S contains strings that are not states). G7 states no theorem about these rules, for example that their models are exactly the multiway systems. STATED.

### C-6 Hypergraph state equivalence (line 526)

"In the Wolfram model these are specified via approximate hypergraph isomorphisms, which are based on extensions of standard error-tolerant graph matching algorithms [bunke1998error], [gorard2016uniqueness]". An approximate isomorphism test identifies some non-isomorphic states. G4 cites the second reference (the uniqueness-trees paper, G4 reference [88]) for its isomorphism tests (`STATUS_OF_GORARD_CLAIMS.md` O8). CITED.

### C-7 Wolfram model rules that build a category and a groupoid from a graph (lines 562-595)

> "the operations stated above can be explicitly expressed as the following Wolfram model rules applied to any initial graph G: {{a,b},{b,c}} → {{a,b},{b,c},{a,c}}, {{a}} → {{a,a}}, where the first rule refers to transitivity and the second to reflexivity." Line 592: {{a,b}} → {{b,a}} is "the additional Wolfram model rule ensuring isomorphisms".

CONTRADICTED. MEASURED (`homotopy_category_rules.py`, initial graph the directed path {{1,2},{2,3},{3,4}}, whose transitive closure has 6 edges):

- The reflexivity rule matches unary hyperedges only. A graph has none, and the rule makes 0 events in 5 steps. If a unary edge {a} were present, the rule would replace it by {a,a}, not add a self-loop at every vertex.
- The transitivity rule under "Generational" -> True gives 4, 5, 7, 9, 11 and 13 edges after steps 1 to 6, with 6 distinct edges from step 3 on and edge multiplicities up to 5. The rule adds {a,c} whether or not it is present, so the evolution never stops and the edge multiset never equals the transitive closure.
- The inverse rule consumes {a,b} and produces {b,a}. It preserves the edge count (3 in every state of the multiway system), and no state contains both orientations of an edge. It does not add inverse edges.

### C-8 Proposition p3.4: the limiting rulial multiverse yields the (∞,1)-category ∞Grpd (lines 874-881)

The proof states that the limiting rulial multiway systems realise ∞-groupoids (Proposition p3.3), that "these ∞-categories are among the objects of the full rulial multiverse, which thus internally realizes ∞Grpd", and that ∞Grpd is equivalent to complete Segal space objects (Lurie). A collection of some ∞-groupoids is not ∞Grpd: ∞Grpd has every ∞-groupoid as an object and its mapping spaces as morphisms, and G7 defines no morphisms between multiway systems (Definition at lines 868-871 defines the rulial multiverse as a "collection"). The proof also rests on Proposition p3.3, whose limit is not defined (G6 C-9). PROVED IN PAPER, proof fails.

### C-9 The rulial multiverse is an (∞,1)-topos (abstract line 83; lines 891, 1032, 1056, 1122)

The abstract states that the classifying space of rulial multiway systems "carries the formal structure of an (∞,1)-topos". The body gives Remark (line 891), "The ∞Grpd is often used as the archetypical example to define an (∞,1)-topos", and at line 1032 "presumably this construction yields an (∞,1)-Grothendieck fibration; this issue will be formally explored in an upcoming work". STATED.

### C-10 Strict versus weak ∞-groupoids and the homotopy hypothesis (lines 829-831, 853-856, 995)

- Remark (line 830): "n-fold groupoids model all homotopy n-types". STATED without a reference.
- Remark (line 855): "The ∞-groupoids discussed here have been constructed within the model of strict n-categories", while the homotopy hypothesis is formulated for weak or quasi-categories, and "there is indication that such definitions or statements will eventually be expressible within other models". READ: G7 states that its construction is strict and that the hypothesis it invokes is formulated for weak models. Strict globular ω-groupoids are equivalent to crossed complexes (Brown and Higgins, 1981) and do not model all homotopy types (Simpson, 1998, for the 3-type of S²).
- Line 995: "If Grothendieck's hypothesis is true, it then provides rigorous justification for the claim that the limiting rulial multiway system is itself a topological space". STATED, conditional on the hypothesis.

### C-11 Geometry without background assumptions (abstract line 83; lines 1034, 1054-1061, 1102-1104, 1127)

"A consequence of constructing spaces and geometry synthetically is that it removes the need to make ad hoc assumptions about geometric attributes of a model" (abstract). Lines 1102-1104: "graph and hypergraph limits often assumed to exist in discrete spacetime models are well-defined only when relevant homotopical structures are admissible within those models". G7 constructs no metric, dimension or continuum limit for any rewriting system, and gives no example of a rule whose limiting rulial multiway system has a stated topology. STATED.

### C-12 Completion as measurement (lines 1072-1094)

Definition (line 1081): a completion is a rule "that brings [a multiway system] closer to causal invariance", with no measure of closeness. Line 1092: "In [Gorard2020a], it was hypothesized that completion procedures such as Knuth-Bendix completions could be used to model measurement". [Gorard2020a] is G5, which states this as proved (G5 lines 615-618, 801-802; `cs.29.2.537_close_reading.md` C-6). Lines 1092-1094: higher homotopies give "a formal and computational procedure for implementing completions". STATED.

### C-13 Univalence (lines 1041-1046)

"implementation of homotopies or completion procedures in multiway systems correspond precisely to propositional extensionality in type theory", and the univalence axiom "provides the formal backing for the operations of equating states and paths". STATED.

### C-14 Figure of cubes (lines 1106-1111)

The graph rules of the cube figure (gluing cubes, subdividing cubes) are described in words, and the statement that the subdividing rule "limits to something that 'fills' the initial cube" is not proved. STATED.
