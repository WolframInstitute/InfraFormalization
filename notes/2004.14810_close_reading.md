# Close reading of JGORARD, "Some Relativistic and Gravitational Properties of the Wolfram Model" (arXiv:2004.14810)

Numbered claims and an assessment of whether the stated argument constitutes a proof.

## §2 — Space, time, causality (pp. 5–25)

### 2.1 Basic definitions
- **Def 1** (p. 5): Spatial hypergraph H=(V,E), E ⊆ P(V)\{∅}, **undirected**. Comment on "directed hypergraphs" appears later (§3.1) without a clean re-definition — see note on **D-1**.
- **Def 3** (p. 6): Update rule: H₁ → H₂ with the same number of "outgoing hyperedges" and "preserving symmetries of H₁". "Outgoing" is undefined for the undirected hypergraph of Def 1, and "preserving symmetries" is not formalised. This is the **first informal-definition hole**; any Lean formalisation needs to commit to directed or undirected hypergraphs consistently, and replace "outgoing hyperedges / symmetries" with a precise matching / pushout formulation.
- **Def 4** (p. 6): Causal graph. "Edge A → B iff update rule at B was only applicable as a result of the outcome of A." Pragmatic rephrase: "non-trivial overlap with output of A." Neither is formal — needs a precise definition of "applicable because of" in terms of which hyperedges are consumed / produced.

### 2.2 Abstract rewriting + causal invariance
- **Defs 5–8** (pp. 8–10): Standard ARS, confluence, Church–Rosser. Fine.
- **Def 9** (p. 10): Causal invariance = all causal graphs from every path of the multiway graph become isomorphic eventually. Prose-level definition only; "eventually isomorphic as DAGs" never precisely quantified ("for some step N past which all causal graphs up to that step are isomorphic"? "after equivalence classes stabilise"? these are different).

### 2.3 Causal graph foliations + ADM (pp. 12–19)
- **Def 15** (p. 14): Discrete spacetime = pair (M, g), M a "discrete metric space", g "a discrete Lorentzian metric of signature (−+++)". **g is never constructed** — it is *declared* and then used in subsequent equations as if extracted. This is the **metric-is-declared** move.
- **Eqs 25–28** (pp. 16): Discrete ADM line element Δs² = (−α² + βⁱβᵢ)Δt² + 2βᵢΔxⁱΔt + γᵢⱼΔxⁱΔxʲ, with identifications
  - α ↔ "effective number of updating events required to map between an input subhypergraph and the corresponding output subhypergraph in two neighbouring hypersurfaces";
  - βⁱ ↔ "effective graph distance between those corresponding subhypergraphs";
  - γᵢⱼ ↔ a "3-dimensional discrete spatial metric" on each hypersurface (never constructed).
  This is a **verbal identification**, not a derivation. The correspondence between the choice of update order and the ADM gauge (α, βⁱ) is at most a *motivating* analogy; there is no theorem here. To prove it one would need: (i) a constructive map from update orderings to (α, βⁱ)-functions on the causal graph, (ii) a uniqueness statement mod equivalence, (iii) a continuum-limit statement that this converges to the ADM lapse/shift on the limiting Lorentzian manifold.

- Claim (p. 19): Causal invariance ⇔ discrete general covariance.
  **Verdict: tautology.** Causal invariance is *defined* as update-order-independence of the causal graph; "general covariance" is here *defined* as foliation/update-order-independence of observable outcomes. These are the same sentence in different clothes. For this to become a substantive theorem one would need an independent notion of diffeomorphism invariance on the continuum limit, and then prove the discrete hypothesis implies it.

- Claim (p. 19): "flat hypersurface foliation" ⇒ Lorentz covariance, and the constancy of c is "enforced axiomatically by our definition of the edge lengths in causal graphs, this completes the proof."
  **Verdict: circular.** The speed of light has been built into the model by fiat (all edges unit length and timelike-separation defined using discrete Minkowski norm of Def 12). No physical content has been derived; the claim "we get SR" is immediate from the axioms, which *are* SR written discretely.

### 2.4 Discrete Lorentz transformations (pp. 20–25)
- **Def 26** (p. 21): Discrete Lorentz transformation t' = cosh(ρ)t − sinh(ρ) x·u. Just the continuum formula, applied to integer coordinates, without any proof that the resulting integer-coordinate set is stable under composition or that it approximates SO⁺(1,n) in any limit.
- Pages 23–25: Kuratowski-subdivision argument about nonplanar tangles as particles; de Bruijn–index "mass" argument. Speculative. No formal statement that can be evaluated as true/false.

## §3 — Discrete geometry and gravity (pp. 26–40) — THE CORE GR DERIVATION

### 3.1 Discrete Ricci scalar via Ollivier–Ricci
- Eqs 47–48: Classical Riemannian ball-volume and transported-ball-average-distance expansions (these are Gray 1973; correct).
- **Def 31** (p. 27): Ollivier–Ricci scalar curvature κ(p,q) = 1 − W₁(m_p, m_q)/d(p,q).
  **Statement in paper:** "In the particular case in which (X,d) is a Riemannian manifold, and m is the standard Riemannian volume measure, the Ollivier–Ricci scalar curvature κ(p,q) reduces (up to some arbitrary scaling factor) to the standard Riemannian Ricci scalar curvature R(p,q)."
  **This is wrong as stated.** Ollivier's theorem (Ollivier, JFA 2009) says that κ(p,q) for close p,q and an ε-ball random walk approaches Ric(v,v)/n ε²/2 (up to O(ε³)), where v is the *tangent vector* at p pointing to q. So κ recovers the *directional Ricci curvature* Ric(v,v), **not** the scalar curvature R. The statement "R(p,q)" doesn't even name a standard object.
- **Def 33** (p. 28): JGORARD's generalisation: an Ollivier–Ricci scalar attached to a *directed hyperedge* via particular in/out probability measures (Eqs 58–59). The construction is ad hoc; no theorem is given that this converges (in any limit) to a continuum Ricci curvature of an embedding manifold.
- Eq 63: N(r) = a rⁿ for "flat" hypergraph, with n the "dimensionality". **"Flat" is never defined** before it is used.
- Eq 65: N(r) = a rⁿ (1 − R r²/(6(n+2)) + O(r⁴)) — **obtained "by analogy"** from the Riemannian formula, **not derived**.

### 3.2 Discrete Riemann / Ricci tensor
- Eqs 66–76: Standard Riemannian review (correct).
- **The critical fiat** (p. 34): "In the case of a (directed) hypergraph, one can set, as usual, all hyperedges to be of unit length, and hence one can also take ε = δ = 1 in all of the definitions given above. Geodesics in the hypergraph are then given by solutions to the shortest paths problem. **We also assume that all pairs of hyperedges which are not parallel (i.e. incident to the same set of vertices) can be considered to be orthonormal.**"
  - This is unjustified on two counts:
    1. **Orthonormality is declared, not constructed.** There is no inner product on "tangent vectors at a vertex" here; the "orthonormality" is a labelling move, not a geometric fact. Without a bilinear form, declaring two objects orthonormal is vacuous.
    2. **ε = δ = 1 invalidates the asymptotic expansion.** The Riemannian formulas (47), (76), (79)–(80) are *asymptotic* in ε → 0. Setting ε = 1 keeps only the leading term of a divergent-in-general expansion. One would need either (a) a rescaling argument (take graphs with many edges, coarsen so ε is effectively small) or (b) a different, non-asymptotic definition. Neither is given.
- Eqs 81–84: sectional → Riemann → Ricci via the fiat-orthonormality; C(t) = a tⁿ (1 − R_jk tʲtᵏ/6 + ...) — **again "by analogy"**.

### 3.3 Discrete Einstein field equations (**the claimed main theorem**)
- Hypothesis: "asymptotic dimensionality preservation" — the causal-graph dimension-anomaly correction → 0 as the causal graph grows.
- Global action (Eq 86): S[gᵃᵇ] = Σ dμ_g R — a discrete sum of JGORARD's discrete Ollivier–Ricci scalar against a "volume element" dμ_g never defined on the discrete object (dμ_g was defined in §3.2 only inside the Riemannian expansion).
- Condition (Eq 87): δS/δgᵃᵇ → 0. **δ/δgᵃᵇ is never defined on a discrete metric.** g has not been constructed as an object with components gᵃᵇ that one could vary.
- Eq 88–91: "in the continuum limit, S = ∫ d⁴x √(−g) R" (Einstein–Hilbert action); varying gives R_ab − (1/2) R g_ab = 0.
  **Verdict: vacuous as a derivation.** The entire content is "we define a thing that looks like the EH action in the continuum, assume it has a well-defined continuum limit, and assume its variation vanishes in that limit." Steps (i) well-defined discrete action, (ii) its continuum limit exists and equals EH, (iii) the vanishing of the discrete analogue of δ/δg corresponds to the vanishing of δS_EH/δg — **none are proved**.

### 3.3 (matter) and 3.4 (varying dimension)
- Eq 97: R_ab − (1/2) R g_ab = 8π T_ab written down; T_ab = −(C_M/(8π√−g)) (∂L_M/∂gᵃᵇ). Standard Hilbert variation; conditional on a matter Lagrangian L_M on the hypergraph being given, which it isn't.
- Eq 100: Adds 2Λ as a literal integration constant, claims cosmological constant emerges. In the continuum this is trivial; the nontrivial question of why the discrete model prefers a specific tiny Λ is handwaved to later sections.

## Summary of the gap structure

| Claim | Status | What would make it a theorem |
|---|---|---|
| Causal invariance ⇒ general covariance | Tautological | Independent continuum definition of GC; prove discrete⇒continuum version holds |
| Causal invariance ⇒ Lorentz covariance | Circular (c built into axioms) | Remove the edge-length axiom, derive a metric, recover Lorentz in a limit |
| Ollivier–Ricci κ ↔ scalar Ricci R | False as stated; mixes directional and scalar | Use directional version, state Ollivier's theorem correctly |
| Non-parallel hyperedges are "orthonormal" | Fiat; no inner product exists | Construct a metric-measure structure, prove orthogonality is consistent |
| ε = δ = 1 in asymptotic expansion | Category error | Rescaling / coarsening argument, or non-asymptotic definition |
| Discrete volume-anomaly sum = EH action in limit | Not defined | Precise continuum-limit functional, prove convergence (e.g. Γ-convergence) |
| δS/δgᵃᵇ → 0 ⇒ vacuum Einstein | g never constructed; variation never defined | Define a discrete metric (bond-lengths + signature), define variational calculus on graphs, prove consistency |

The pattern is systematic: every time the argument needs to cross between discrete and continuum, JGORARD writes down the continuum formula, replaces integrals by sums and derivatives by differences "by analogy," and proceeds without proving the correspondence. None of §3 constitutes a proof of the discrete Einstein equations; what is there is a *motivating plausibility argument* that one might *hope* to make rigorous.

## Formal program (what we need in Lean to actually prove or refute the main theorem)

1. A rigorous definition of a *discrete metric-measure Lorentzian structure* (discrete (M, g, μ)) with explicit Euler and Lorentz signatures.
2. A rigorous definition of the discrete Ollivier–Ricci tensor (not just scalar) for directed hyperedges, with a proven convergence theorem to the continuum Ricci tensor under a specified sequence of coarsenings / embeddings.
3. A rigorous definition of the discrete Einstein–Hilbert functional, and a Γ-convergence (or weak variational) result showing it converges to ∫ √(−g) R d⁴x on the continuum limit.
4. A discrete-to-continuum metric variation principle (which is the genuinely hard step — "δ/δg on a graph" needs defining).
5. A theorem: for causal-invariant + asymptotically-dimension-preserving hypergraph rewriting systems admitting a continuum limit in the sense of (1)–(4), the limit metric satisfies R_ab − (1/2) R g_ab = 0.

Any item we cannot define or prove at the required level of precision becomes either (a) an explicit hypothesis we must add (and flag as un-verified for JGORARD's original claim), or (b) a counterexample we can construct.
