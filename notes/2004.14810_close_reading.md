# Close reading of J. Gorard, "Some Relativistic and Gravitational Properties of the Wolfram Model" (arXiv:2004.14810)

This note lists the definitions and claims of G1 with their line numbers in `papers/gorard_2004.14810.txt`, and states for each what the text defines and what argument it gives. Every finding is READ unless another status is given. The repository's Lean files for findings F1-F5 are described in [`MAJOR_FLAWS.md`](../MAJOR_FLAWS.md) and [`STATUS_OF_GORARD_CLAIMS.md`](../STATUS_OF_GORARD_CLAIMS.md).

## §2. Space, time and causality (lines 216-1422)

### §2.1 Basic definitions (lines 218-357)

- Def 1 (line 233): a spatial hypergraph H = (V, E) is finite and undirected, with E ⊆ P(V) \ {∅}. Directed hypergraphs, with tail and head sets, appear at line 1577 without a revised Def 1.
- Def 3 (lines 274-278): an update rule H₁ → H₂ replaces H₁ by a subhypergraph "with the same number of outgoing hyperedges as H₁, and in such a way as to preserve any symmetries of H₁". G1 does not define "outgoing" for the undirected hypergraphs of Def 1, and does not state which symmetries are preserved.
- Def 4 (lines 288-291): the causal edge A → B exists "if and only if the update rule designated by event B was only applicable as a result of the outcome of the update rule designated by event A". Lines 296-298 restate this as a "non-trivial overlap" between the input of B and the output of A. Neither form names the consumed and produced hyperedges. The repository defines a causal graph on hyperedge identities in `lean/InfraFormalization/CausalHistory.lean`.

### §2.2 Abstract rewriting and causal invariance (lines 358-629)

- Defs 5-8 (lines 367-420): abstract rewriting systems, reflexive transitive closure, confluence and the Church-Rosser property. These agree with the standard definitions (Baader and Nipkow).
- Def 9 (lines 458-462): a multiway system is causal invariant if its causal graphs "are all, eventually, isomorphic as directed, acyclic graphs". G1 does not state the sense of "eventually". Isomorphism of the finite causal graphs after some step and isomorphism of the infinite limits are two different readings.

### §2.3 Hypersurfaces, ADM decomposition and covariance (lines 630-1146)

- Def 15 (lines 786-788): a discrete spacetime is a pair (M, g), with M a discrete metric space and g "a discrete Lorentzian metric of signature (− + ++)". G1 gives no procedure that computes g from the hypergraph or the causal graph.
- Eqs. 25-28 (lines 851-886): the discrete ADM line element Δs² = (−α² + βⁱβᵢ)Δt² + 2βᵢΔxⁱΔt + γᵢⱼΔxⁱΔxʲ. Lines 889-899 state that "under this formal analogy" the choice of α and βⁱ corresponds to the choice of updating order, with α the "effective number of updating events required to map between an input subhypergraph and the corresponding output subhypergraph in two neighboring hypersurfaces" and βⁱ the "effective graph distance between those corresponding subhypergraphs". The spatial metric γᵢⱼ is not constructed. G1 gives no map from updating orders to functions α, βⁱ on the causal graph, and no convergence statement to the lapse and shift of a Lorentzian manifold.
- Lines 1063-1075: causal invariance corresponds to the claim that "the eventual outcomes of updating events are independent of the reference frame", which G1 calls "a discretized version of the principle of general covariance". Both notions are stated as independence from the updating order. The repository encodes both as `∀ u v, cg u = cg v` and proves their equivalence by `Iff.rfl` (`causalInvariance_iff_discreteGeneralCovariance`, `Refutations/F4Tautology.lean:51`, PROVED, no axioms). The identification of the two notions in G1 is the READ finding.
- Lines 1079-1088: with "flat" hypersurfaces, causal invariance "reduces to a statement of discrete Lorentz covariance", and the "constancy of the speed of light - is enforced axiomatically by our definition of the edge lengths in causal graphs, this completes the proof". The constancy of the speed of light is part of the definitions (the discrete Minkowski norm of Def 12, line 682, and unit causal edge lengths). Line 1081 refers forward to §3 for the definition of curvature of a discrete hypersurface.

### §2.4 Discrete Lorentz transformations (lines 1147-1422)

- Def 26 (lines 1181-1188, eq. 43): t′ = cosh(ρ) t − sinh(ρ) x·u, with x·u the inner product on Zⁿ. G1 does not state whether the map sends integer coordinates to integer coordinates, whether the maps compose, or in what limit they approximate SO⁺(1, n).
- Lines 1290-1420: nonplanar "tangles" in spatial graphs (Kuratowski's theorem, Defs 27-28) are proposed as models of particles, and de Bruijn indices of λ-terms (lines 1335-1420) as a measure of relativistic mass. These passages state interpretations and contain no proposition with a proof.

## §3. Discrete geometry and gravitation (lines 1423-2612)

### §3.1 Ollivier-Ricci scalar curvature (lines 1427-1813)

- Eq. 47 (lines 1436-1447): the Riemannian ball-volume ratio 1 − R ε²/(6(n+2)) + O(ε⁴). This agrees with Gray (1973).
- Eq. 48 (lines 1455-1469): the average distance between transported ε-balls is δ(1 − ε²R/(2(n+2)) + O(ε³ + ε²δ)), with R the scalar curvature. Ollivier (2009, Example 7) gives the same expansion with Ric(v,v), the Ricci curvature in the direction v of the geodesic from p to q, in place of R. The two coincide only where Ric(v,v) = R. G1 eq. 80 (line 2054) and G3 eq. 344 use the R form. The comparison is PROVED as arithmetic in `Infra.ollivier_gorard_agree_iff` and recorded in [`results/phase2_continuum/eq344_verdict.md`](../results/phase2_continuum/eq344_verdict.md).
- Def 31 (lines 1521-1534, eq. 51): κ(p, q) = 1 − W₁(m_p, m_q)/d(p, q). Lines 1530-1534 state that on a Riemannian manifold with the volume measure κ(p, q) "reduces (up to some arbitrary scaling factor) to the standard Riemannian Ricci scalar curvature R (p, q)". Ollivier (2009, Example 7) gives κ(p, q) = ε² Ric(v,v)/(2(n+2)) + O(ε³ + ε² d(p, q)) for the uniform measure on ε-balls, with v the unit tangent vector from p to q. Ric(v,v) depends on the direction v and the scalar curvature R does not. G1 does not define the two-point quantity R(p, q).
- Def 33 (lines 1592-1680, eqs. 55-59): an Ollivier-Ricci curvature κ(e) = 1 − W(μ_A^in, μ_B^out) for a directed hyperedge e: A → B, with measures built from in-degrees and out-degrees. G1 gives no convergence theorem relating κ(e) to the Ricci curvature of a manifold.
- Eq. 63 (lines 1733-1739): N(r) = a rⁿ for a "flat" spatial hypergraph. G1 does not define "flat" for a hypergraph at this point.
- Eqs. 64-65 (lines 1772-1798): "Using the fact that the Ollivier-Ricci scalar curvature reduces to the standard Riemannian Ricci scalar curvature" (line 1772), G1 states the ratio 1 − R r²/(6(n+2)) + O(r⁴) for discrete balls "for any sufficiently small value of r (with exactness when r = 1)" (line 1790), and N(r) = a rⁿ(1 − R r²/(6(n+2)) + O(r⁴)). This is eq. 47 with ε replaced by the integer radius r. G1 gives no derivation for hypergraphs. The smallest positive radius in a hypergraph is r = 1.

### §3.2 Sectional, Riemann and Ricci curvature (lines 1814-2167)

- Eqs. 66-79 (lines 1823-2025): standard Riemannian definitions (Christoffel symbols, Riemann tensor, sectional curvature, volume element eq. 76). These agree with the standard references.
- Eq. 80 (lines 2045-2057): the transported-ball distance with scalar curvature R, the same form as eq. 48, stated to hold "as ε, δ → 0" (line 2057).
- Lines 2059-2065: "In the case of a (directed) hypergraph, one can set, as usual, all hyperedges to be of unit length, and hence one can also take ε = δ = 1 in all of the definitions given above. ... We also assume that all pairs of hyperedges which are not parallel (i.e. incident to the same set of vertices) can be considered to be orthonormal."
  - Orthonormality is defined relative to a bilinear form. G1 defines no bilinear form on hyperedges or on directions at a vertex. The Lean file `Refutations/F2Orthonormality.lean` is VACUOUS with respect to G1 (it concerns forms on `Bool`).
  - Eqs. 47, 48, 76, 79 and 80 are expansions as ε, δ → 0. G1 sets ε = δ = 1 and keeps the leading terms. G1 gives no rescaling argument and no non-asymptotic definition for ε = δ = 1.
- Eqs. 81-84 (lines 2077-2140): the sectional curvature K(u, v) = ⟨R(u, v)v, u⟩ under the orthonormality assumption, the flat cone count C(t) = a tⁿ (eq. 82, "by direct analogy to the spatial hypergraph case", line 2098), and C(t) = a tⁿ(1 − R_jk tʲtᵏ/6 + O(‖t‖³)) (eq. 84), obtained from the Riemannian volume element eq. 83 "by analogy to the purely spatial case" (line 2109).

### §3.3 Discrete Einstein field equations (lines 2168-2612)

- Hypothesis (lines 2171-2184): "asymptotic dimensionality preservation", stated as convergence to zero of the growth rate of the "global dimension anomaly" as the causal graph grows.
- Eq. 86 (line 2200): S[g^ab] = Σ dμ_g R, a sum of the discrete Ollivier-Ricci scalar against a volume element dμ_g. G1 introduces dμ_g only in the Riemannian expansion eq. 76 and does not define it on the causal graph.
- Eq. 87 (lines 2203-2211): asymptotic dimensionality preservation "corresponds to the statement" δS[g^ab]/δg^ab → 0. G1 does not define the functional derivative of a discrete sum with respect to the metric of Def 15, which it does not construct. The condition δS/δg^ab = 0 is the Euler-Lagrange equation of S. The repository encodes both sides as `P.variation g = 0` and proves their equivalence by `Iff.rfl` (`asymptoticDimPreservation_iff_eulerLagrange`, `Refutations/F3Tautology.lean:57`, PROVED). The encoding of eq. 87 is the READ finding.
- Eq. 88 (lines 2214-2225): "In the continuum limit of an arbitrarily large causal graph, this sum becomes (subject to a weak ergodicity assumption on the dynamics of the causal graph) an integral", S = ∫ d⁴x √−g R. G1 does not state a notion of convergence and does not define weak ergodicity.
- Eqs. 89-91 (lines 2239-2259): the variation of the Einstein-Hilbert action gives R_ab − ½ R g_ab = 0. This agrees with the standard derivation. Lines 2262-2266 state the conclusion for rules that are causal invariant and asymptotically dimensionality preserving, "if a continuum limit exists in which the causal graph becomes a Riemannian manifold".
- G1 gives no proof of three steps of the argument: (i) a discrete action defined on the causal graph, (ii) convergence of that action to the Einstein-Hilbert action, (iii) correspondence between the discrete condition of eq. 87 and the vanishing of δS_EH/δg^ab.
- Eqs. 96-99 (lines 2349-2377): a matter term L = L_G + C_M L_M, with C_M "an arbitrary constant", gives R_ab − ½ R g_ab = 8π T_ab with T_ab = −(C_M/(8π√−g)) ∂L_M/∂g^ab. This is the standard Hilbert variation. G1 describes matter through the count of edges in nonplanar tangles that cross a hypersurface (lines 2327-2348) and does not define L_M as a function on the hypergraph.
- Eqs. 100-102 (lines 2389-2416): adding an arbitrary constant 2Λ to the summand gives R_ab − ½ R g_ab + Λ g_ab = 8π T_ab, and G1 concludes that the model "is consistent with both zero and non-zero values of the cosmological constant" (lines 2419-2421). G1 does not derive a value of Λ.

## Summary

| Claim | Lines | What G1 gives | What a proof would need |
|---|---|---|---|
| Causal invariance corresponds to discrete general covariance | 1063-1075 | Both notions stated as updating-order independence | A continuum notion of diffeomorphism invariance and a proof that the discrete property implies it |
| Causal invariance with flat foliations gives Lorentz covariance | 1079-1088 | The speed of light fixed by the definition of edge lengths | A metric derived from the hypergraph and Lorentz covariance recovered in a limit |
| κ(p, q) reduces to the scalar curvature | 1530-1534 | Statement without proof | Ollivier (2009) gives Ric(v,v), which depends on the direction |
| Non-parallel hyperedges are orthonormal | 2063-2065 | Assumption | A bilinear form on hyperedges |
| ε = δ = 1 in expansions valid as ε, δ → 0 | 2059-2061 | Assumption | A rescaling argument or a non-asymptotic definition |
| Σ dμ_g R becomes ∫ √−g R | 2214-2225 | Statement "subject to a weak ergodicity assumption" | A notion of convergence and a proof of convergence |
| δS/δg^ab → 0 gives the vacuum Einstein equations | 2203-2266 | Variation of a metric that is not constructed | A discrete metric, a discrete variational calculus and a proof that the discrete condition converges to the continuum one |

## Statements a Lean proof of the §3.3 result would need

1. A discrete Lorentzian metric-measure structure (M, g, μ) computed from the causal graph.
2. A discrete Ollivier-Ricci tensor for directed hyperedges, with a convergence theorem to the Ricci tensor of a manifold along a stated sequence of hypergraphs.
3. A discrete Einstein-Hilbert functional and a convergence theorem (for example Γ-convergence) to ∫ √−g R d⁴x.
4. A definition of variation with respect to the discrete metric.
5. The theorem: for causal-invariant, asymptotically dimension-preserving rules with a limit in the sense of 1-4, the limit metric satisfies R_ab − ½ R g_ab = 0.

All five are OPEN. A definition that cannot be given becomes an explicit hypothesis of the formal statement.
