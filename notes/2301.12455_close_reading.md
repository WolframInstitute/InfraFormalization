# Close reading of J. Gorard and J. Dannemann-Freitag, "Axiomatic Quantum Field Theory in Discrete Spacetime via Multiway Causal Structure: The Case of Entanglement Entropies" (arXiv:2301.12455)

The paper is called G9 in this repository. All line citations refer to the TeX source `papers/tex_2301_12455/EntanglementEntropy.tex` (2284 lines). The text extraction is `papers/gorard_2301.12455.txt`. The classes are those of [`cs.29.2.537_close_reading.md`](cs.29.2.537_close_reading.md). G9's §2-§4 concern causal sets sprinkled into Minkowski space, with Green's functions, an analytic continuation in the dimension and the Sorkin-Johnston entropy. §5 concerns Wolfram model evolutions. The claims below are those about hypergraph rewriting, multiway systems, branchial graphs, dimension and continuum limits, and the formulas G9 states for them.

## 2. Claims

### C-1 Abstract: what G9 shows (line 28)

"we show how an axiomatic field theory for a free, massless scalar field ... may be rigorously constructed over multiway causal graphs ... We proceed to show numerically that the entanglement entropies computed using both the Sorkin-Johnston approach and the branchial graph approach are monotonically related for a large class of Wolfram model evolution rules. We also prove a special case of this monotonic relationship using a recent geometrical entanglement monotone proposed by Cocchiarella et al." See C-10 to C-13.

### C-2 Wolfram model causal graphs have the limiting properties of causal sets (lines 37, 1723-1762)

- Line 37: the causal graph is a partially ordered set "with the same limiting properties as a causal set". STATED.
- Line 37 and line 1762: the transitive reduction of a causal graph is the Hasse diagram of the corresponding causal set. Correct for every finite directed acyclic graph.
- Line 1726: hypergraphs with E ⊆ P(V) \ {∅}, with "finite collections of (ordered) relations" (line 1739) and the initial state {{0,0},{0,0}} (line 1733). The definition excludes ordered edges, repeated vertices and repeated edges, which the initial state has. READ (the definition of G1, G5, G7 and G8).
- Line 1762: causal invariance is "the discrete analog of general covariance", with the causal graph "always isomorphic, irrespective of which path through the multiway system ... is chosen". CITED ([gorard] = G1, [gorard3] = G2, [gorard4] = G3).
- Line 1762: isomorphic states are merged "using a generalized version of the 'uniqueness trees' algorithm [gorard9]". CITED (Gorard 2016; O8 of `STATUS_OF_GORARD_CLAIMS.md`).

### C-3 Multiway systems are dagger-symmetric compact-closed monoidal categories (lines 37, 1778)

"The resulting multiway system possesses the algebraic structure of a dagger-symmetric, compact-closed monoidal category [gorard5][gorard6]", with the compact structure "the swapping of the vertex and hyperedge sets". CITED to the two ZX-calculus papers (arXiv:2010.02752, 2103.15820). The same hypergraph dual is stated in G8 without unit or counit morphisms (`2301.04690_close_reading.md` C-14).

### C-4 Branchial graphs and amplitudes (lines 1788-1824)

- Line 1788: the branchial edge A ↔ B exists "if and only if the corresponding hypergraph states ... share some common ancestor C". With one initial state every pair of states has the initial state as a common ancestor, so this graph is complete. G5 (lines 1508-1510), HGEvolve and the repository's Lean definition `BranchialRelated` (`lean/InfraFormalization/Branchial.lean`: ∃ s, Step sys s s₁ ∧ Step sys s s₂) join states with a common immediate predecessor. READ.
- Line 1788: each state "may be assigned a numerical weight based on the number of distinct evolution paths ... interpreted as a (magnitude of a) quantum amplitude [gorard2]". The example (lines 1790-1824) applies √NOT = ½[[1+i, 1−i],[1−i, 1+i]] to (|0⟩ + |1⟩)/√2. CONTRADICTED. MEASURED (`entanglement_rootnot.py`): (|0⟩ + |1⟩)/√2 is an eigenvector of √NOT with eigenvalue 1, so its amplitude moduli stay (0.7071, 0.7071) and agree with the normalised path counts at every step. From |0⟩ the path counts are equal at every step t ≥ 1 (both basis states are successors of both), while √NOT²|0⟩ = NOT|0⟩ = |1⟩ has moduli (0, 1). The normalised counts differ from the moduli by 0.7071 at t = 2, 4, 6. Path counts are positive and cannot represent the cancellation in √NOT².

### C-5 Convergence of the branchial metric to the Fubini-Study metric (lines 41, 1828-1843)

CITED to G5 and the first ZX paper. G5's argument for this convergence (G5 §3.2) consists of stated steps and states no limit object or mode of convergence (`cs.29.2.537_close_reading.md` C-17). ### C-7 The two example rules have 1+1-dimensional limiting causal graphs (lines 813, 821, 975, 996-1018)

> "The computations of the first-order logarithmic difference estimator Δ_t(p) ... for (directed) causal graphs which limit to be asymptotically-flat and asymptotically-negatively-curved 1+1-dimensional Lorentzian manifold-like structures are shown in Figures 10 and 12, respectively (with the limiting dimension of two identified correctly in both cases)" (line 975).

The rules are {{x,y,y},{x,z,u}} → {{u,v,v},{v,z,y},{x,y,v}} and {{x,y,x},{x,z,u}} → {{u,v,u},{v,u,z},{x,y,v}}. G9 does not state the initial condition, the update order or the number of events for these figures. CONTRADICTED for the initial states checked. MEASURED (`entanglement_dimension.py`, `entanglement_dimension_multiway.py`):

- From 2 or 3 copies of {0,0,0} (the analogue of the double self-loop G9 uses for every other example), every history applies one event per step, so the causal graph is a chain. Under "Generational" for 30 steps the causal graph has 30 events and 29 causal edges for both rules, and G9's estimator Δ_t on the forward cone of the first event rises from 0.585 at t = 1 to 0.966 at t = 28, the values of a chain (Δ_t = log((t+2)/(t+1))/log((t+1)/t) for cone counts t + 1).
- From 6 copies of {0,0,0}, "Generational" gives 3 events per step in 3 independent chains (90 events, 87 causal edges), with the same Δ_t on the forward cone.
- In the multiway evolution from 2 copies at most 2 events apply to any state, and all events applicable to one state overlap. From {{0,1,1},{0,2,3},{2,1,1}} the first rule applies once and stops, and the second does not apply.

The estimator reads 1 on these causal graphs. The cone-growth estimator is UNCALIBRATED on sprinkled causal sets (`notes/empirical_refutation.md` section 6), and a chain has dimension 1 under every dimension notion for causal sets.

### C-9 Uniqueness of the continuation in the dimension by Carlson's theorem (lines 1061-1118)

The continuation is a sum of polynomials G̃_n(d) = (b + cd) d^m ∏_{k=−(n−1)}^{n−1} (d − k), with m chosen so that |G̃_n(d)| < 2^{−n} on |d| ≤ n − 1 (lines 1066-1084). Line 1103: "the polynomials G̃_n(d) and constant m ∈ ℂ have been specifically chosen to ensure that the growth conditions" |f(d)| ≤ C e^{τ|d|} on ℂ and |f(iy)| ≤ C e^{c|y|} with c < π "must be satisfied". The conditions imposed on G̃_n concern the disk |d| ≤ n − 1 only and say nothing about the growth of the sum outside it or along the imaginary axis. Line 1061 calls the continuation meromorphic, while Carlson's theorem as quoted (line 1088) requires a function holomorphic on ℂ. Lines 1061 and 1086 state uniqueness "up to multiples of sin(πd)". sin(πd) has type π on the imaginary axis, which the condition c < π excludes, and the proof concludes that the two continuations are identical. The first continuation of the section (line 1043, a sum of sinc(d − k)^{m_k}) has type π m_k on the imaginary axis and lies outside the class to which the proof applies. PROVED IN PAPER ("This completes the proof", line 1118), proof fails: the growth conditions are asserted.

### C-10 The branchial entanglement measure is an entanglement monotone (lines 1890-1940)

G9 proves that E(|s⟩) = Σ_μ [tr(A^μ) − 2(d_μ − 1)] satisfies 0 ≤ E ≤ Σ_μ 2(d_μ − 1)/d_μ, with E = 0 for fully separable states and the maximum when every reduced state is maximally mixed, and that E is invariant under local unitaries.

- The bounds hold: Σ_k ⟨s|T^{μk}|s⟩² = 2(tr ρ_μ² − 1/d_μ) for the reduced state ρ_μ, which lies between 0 and 2(d_μ − 1)/d_μ. Line 1920 states that the minimum is attained when |s⟩ is "a normalized tensor product of basis vectors". It is attained for every product state. Eq. at line 1903 prints 2(2_μ − 1)/d_μ for the term that equals 2(d_μ − 1)/d_μ after the subtraction.
- Local-unitary invariance holds: conjugation by U^μ acts on the vector (⟨T^{μk}⟩)_k by an orthogonal matrix. The intermediate equation at line 1950 drops the cross terms Σ_k n^k_α n^k_β ⟨T^α⟩⟨T^β⟩ for α ≠ β, which vanish by orthogonality and are not shown to vanish.
- An entanglement monotone does not increase on average under local operations and classical communication. G9 shows bounds and local-unitary invariance only. Line 45 states that the measure "is monotonically-decreasing under local unitary transformations", while the proof shows invariance.

PROVED IN PAPER: the bounds and the local-unitary invariance hold. Monotonicity under local operations is not shown and is CITED to Cocchiarella et al. [cocchiarella].

### C-11 Monotonic relation between E and the branchial metric (lines 1967-1992; abstract "We also prove a special case of this monotonic relationship")

The argument ends with g_μμ(v^μ) = Σ_ij v_i^μ v_j^μ A_ij^μ (line 1990), "hence justifying the existence of a monotonic relationship between the trace of the matrices A^μ (and therefore the entanglement monotone E) and the limiting Fubini-Study metric g(v) on branchial graphs". This identity is the result of Cocchiarella et al. relating the Fubini-Study metric on the orbit of local unitaries to the matrices A^μ. It relates a quadratic form in A^μ to the metric along directions v^μ. It does not relate the graph distance between two vertices of a branchial graph, which is the quantity of the numerical comparison (line 1994), to E, and it states no monotone function. The unit vectors n^μ are said to lie in ℝ^{d_μ} (line 1977), while the generators number d_μ² − 1. PROVED IN PAPER, proof fails: the monotonic relation is not derived.
