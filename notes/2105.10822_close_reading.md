## 2. Claims

### C-1 Abstract (line 65)

"We demonstrate the existence of higher homotopies in this class of rewriting systems", "we show that a multiway rewriting system with homotopies up to order n may naturally be formalized as an n-fold category, such that ... the infinite limit of this structure yields an ∞-groupoid. Via Grothendieck's homotopy hypothesis, this ∞-groupoid thus inherits the structure of a formal homotopy space." The one example in the paper has homotopies of order 2 (Figure 5, lines 198-223). The figures for order 3 (ACTHomotopy7 and ACTHomotopy8) are commented out in the TeX source (lines 227-241). See C-6 to C-9 for the propositions.

### C-2 A labelled abstract rewriting system is a bijective function A → P(Λ × A) (line 112)

> "the system (A, Λ, →) is now simply a bijective function from A to a subset of the power set of A indexed by Λ, i.e. P(Λ × A)"

CONTRADICTED. The map p ↦ {(α, q) : p →α q} sends every normal form to the empty set, so it is not injective on any system with two normal forms. MEASURED: AB → A, BA → B from ABA has the normal forms A and AA (`qm_string_figures.py`, the system of G5 Figure 6). The coalgebra description that follows (lines 117-121, → : A → P A) is the standard one and needs no bijectivity. CITED for the coalgebra description.

### C-3 Rulial space as a category of cospans (Definition at lines 166-168, lines 169)

"The rulial space of Wolfram model systems functorially acquires the structure of a selective adhesive category" and inherits a monoidal structure "as a consequence of the concurrency and parallelism theorems" (line 169). STATED for the selective adhesive structure, CITED ([Gorard2020c], the ZX-calculus paper) for the monoidal structure. The rules of lines 127-131 are spans, and the definition takes cospans. G6 does not state how a span rule gives a cospan.

### C-4 Figure 1 (line 108)

The multiway evolution graph of {{x,y},{y,z}} → {{w,y},{y,z},{z,w},{x,w}} from {{0,0},{0,0}} for 3 steps. MEASURED: HGEvolve with isomorphic states identified gives 1, 1, 3 and 17 states at steps 0 to 3 and 20 distinct parent-child pairs into step 3. An independent brute-force count over all vertex relabellings (`fig1_bruteforce.py`) gives the same numbers. The figure (`ACTHomotopy1.png`) draws 1, 1, 3 and 18 states and 20 edges into step 3. Two of the 18 drawn states are therefore isomorphic as directed hypergraphs, or the figure uses a different notion of state identity, which G6 does not state.

### C-5 The homotopy rules of the string system A → AB (lines 181-223)

- The paths from AA to ABBBABBB in the multiway graph of A → AB are "proofs" of the proposition AA → ABBBABBB (lines 181, 194). MEASURED: there are 20 such paths.
- Lines 198-209: the homotopy maps between the red and yellow paths of Figure 5 are introduced "as a new set of rewrite relations that can be appended to the original multiway system rules", the five rules AAB → ABA, AABB → ABBA, AABBB → ABBBA, ABABBB → ABBBAB, ABBABBB → ABBBABB and their inverses. Appended as string rules, they apply to every state that contains a left-hand side as a substring. MEASURED (`homotopy_figures.py`): on the 28 states with at most 6 B's, the ten rules give 40 edges, of which 30 are not among the ten pairs that join the two highlighted paths (for example AABB → ABAB and AABBB → ABBAB). The proof of Proposition 3.1 (lines 283-286) uses only the ten purple pairs. Figure 5 (ACTHomotopy6.pdf) also draws further rule edges in grey, including inverse-rule arrows. G7 states this in its Figure 5 caption (`papers/tex_2111/homotopy1.tex` line 650: "the new rewriting rules ... are also applicable to other states of the multiway system").

### C-6 Proposition 3.1: the system of Figure 5 is a double category (lines 248-287)

PROVED IN PAPER, proof holds with the following reading. M₀ is the category of paths of the multiway graph. The purple arrows are the horizontal arrows, and "imposing commutativity over squares" defines the squares (line 284). With squares taken as the commutative squares of the category generated by the multiway graph of the extended system and the imposed equations, the data form a double category (the double category of commutative squares of a category). This construction applies to any category, so the statement does not depend on the homotopy rules or on the system of Figure 5. The proof does not verify the double category axioms. Two details of the proof: "Transitive closure along directed paths ensures associativity" (line 280) and "self-loops on nodes (trivially obtained via identity rewrite rules) ensure identity laws" (line 280). In a category of paths, composition is concatenation and identities are the empty paths. A self-loop edge is a non-identity morphism whose powers are distinct paths.

### C-7 Corollary 3.1: with invertible rules the system is a double groupoid (lines 290-296)

"Adding invertible rules to the multiway system equips every rewrite arrow with an inverse arrow, such that their composition is the identity map" (line 296). In the multiway graph, an edge and its reverse edge compose to a path of length 2, which is an identity only after the quotient by the relations e·e⁻¹ = id. The proof assumes this quotient. PROVED IN PAPER, proof fails without the quotient, and holds for the free groupoid on the multiway graph, which exists for every graph.

### C-8 Proposition 3.2: n-fold categories (lines 300-358)

"The proof of this proposition follows by a simple inductive construction" (line 306). Line 308: "In Proposition 3.2, we proved this proposition for the n = 2 case", a reference to the proposition being proved, in place of Proposition 3.1. For n = 3 the proof states that cubes "can be done by adding specific rewrite rules" (line 310) and gives no rules. For general n it describes the objects of M_i as "i-dimensional hypercubes in ℝⁿ whose normal vectors are oriented along the z-axis" (line 355), which are geometric objects and not constructed from a rewriting system. The hypothesis "so long as additional rewrite rules between cells, up to order n − 1, are admissible" has no definition of admissible. PROVED IN PAPER, proof fails: no construction for n ≥ 3.

### C-9 Proposition 3.3: the n → ∞ limit is an ∞-groupoid (lines 379-387)

"The n → ∞ limit of an n-fold groupoid is precisely an ∞-groupoid. This limit exists so long as the infinite hierarchy of rules required to iteratively construct higher morphisms is admissible" (line 386). G6 does not state the diagram whose limit is taken, the category in which it is taken, or which notion of ∞-groupoid is meant. A commented-out remark of the TeX source (line 391) states that "The ∞-groupoids discussed here have been constructed within the model of strict n-categories", and G7 prints this remark (homotopy1.tex line 855). PROVED IN PAPER, proof fails: the limit is not defined.

### C-10 Homotopy hypothesis and spaces of physics (lines 65, 80, 399)

"For specific models of ∞-categories, these structures correspond precisely to topological spaces, hence allowing one to make formal connections between the abstract computational framework ... and the topological spaces (such as Lorentzian manifolds, or projective Hilbert spaces) upon which laws of physics are traditionally instantiated" (line 80). The homotopy hypothesis identifies ∞-groupoids with homotopy types. A homotopy type carries no metric, Lorentzian structure or Hilbert space structure. STATED. Line 399 calls the connection a speculation ("we speculate that this correspondence might potentially offer a new way").
