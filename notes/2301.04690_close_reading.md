## 2. Claims

### C-1 Turing-complete computations are irreducible (line 31)

"it is straightforward to prove by diagonalization that any Turing-complete computation must be irreducible". STATED, no proof. Under the definition G8 adopts (line 49, from Gorard 2018), a computation of f(i) for a fixed input i in n steps is reducible if some machine T* computes f(i) in m < n steps. For a fixed i, the machine that writes the fixed output f(i) and ignores its input runs in a number of steps bounded by a constant plus the length of the output and the head displacement. So under line 49 every computation whose step count exceeds this bound is reducible, whether or not the machine is universal. CONTRADICTED by the definition of line 49.

### C-2 Irreducibility is functoriality (lines 114-160)

The map Z′ sends a configuration X to the step number at which it occurs and a morphism f : X → Y to the interval [Z′(X), Z′(Y)] ∩ ℕ (lines 123-138). Line 128: "since all computations are irreducible ... by hypothesis, the function Z′ : 𝒯 → ℬ trivially forms a functor". Line 160: "Since this functorial relationship holds only in the case where all computations in 𝒯 are irreducible, we are consequently able to conclude that, in some precise sense, computational irreducibility is functoriality".

- The definition of Z′ uses the step numbers of the evolution and no property of any other machine. For a deterministic evolution whose quiver is a path, Z′(g ∘ f) = [t₁, t₃] = Z′(g) ∪ Z′(f) holds for every composable pair, so Z′ is a functor for every such evolution, reducible or not. The hypothesis of irreducibility is not used. When the quiver contains a cycle (line 97), a configuration occurs at several step numbers and Z′ is not well defined on objects.
- The converse, "holds only in the case where all computations ... are irreducible", is given no argument. CONTRADICTED: Z′ is a functor for every evolution whose quiver is a path, including the reducible ones of C-1.

### C-3 ℬ is a cobordism category (lines 169-273)

Lines 171-174 replace the objects of ℬ by the real numbers and the morphisms by closed intervals, and conclude "Thus, ℬ is really a cobordism category". The definition used (lines 176-180) requires all finite coproducts, an initial object ∅ and an additive endofunctor ∂ with ∂∂M = ∅. With one morphism [x, y] for each x ≤ y, ℬ is the poset (ℝ, ≤) as a category. Its coproduct is max and it has no initial object, since ℝ has no least element. G8 does not define ∂ on ℬ. CONTRADICTED by the definition of lines 176-180. With objects ℕ (line 126), 0 is initial and ∂ = the constant functor at 0 satisfies the definition, which the paper does not state.

### C-4 Monoidal structure on 𝒯 with the halting state as unit (lines 332-405)

Line 332: 𝒯 "is now (in the non-deterministic/multiway case) a monoidal category ... as proved for the case of generic multiway systems based on symbolic rewriting in [gorard4] and [gorard5]". The references are the two ZX-calculus papers (arXiv:2010.02752, 2103.15820). Line 405: associativity and unitality are inherited from disjoint union, "with the halt state HALT of the Turing machine playing the role of the unit object I, since ... parallel composition with the halt state does not substantively modify the structure". The objects of 𝒯 are single configurations (line 57: Γ^ℵ₀ × Q × ℕ), so a disjoint union X ⊔ Y of two configurations is not an object of 𝒯, and the unit of disjoint union is the empty collection: HALT ⊔ X has one more component than X. CONTRADICTED by the object set of line 57. CITED for the general claim.

### C-5 Monoidal structure on ℬ (lines 480-563)

ℬ "can trivially be equipped with a commutative tensor product structure (namely the disjoint union of sets and intervals ⊔) with a unit object given by the empty set ∅", and "for more abstract and general cobordism categories, the coproduct ⊕ plays the role of the disjoint union". The coproduct of the poset category (ℕ, ≤) of line 126 is max, not disjoint union, and the empty set is not an object of ℬ, whose objects are natural numbers. With ⊕ = max, Z′(f ⊗ g) = [max(t₁, s₁), max(t₂, s₂)] keeps one interval and loses the other. G8 then uses objects t₁ ⊕ s₁ that are pairs of numbers (lines 548-563), which requires a different category. CONTRADICTED by the paper's own identification of ⊕ with the coproduct.

### C-6 Multicomputational irreducibility is symmetric monoidal functoriality (lines 471-564)

The statement is made under "the additional hypothesis that all parallel compositions of singleway computations are multicomputationally irreducible" (line 480), and line 564 concludes that "multicomputational irreducibility is reflected in the symmetric monoidal functoriality of Z′". The coherence conditions are written out (lines 485-543) and not verified for Z′. The argument has the structure of C-2 and depends on the tensor products of C-4 and C-5. STATED.

### C-7 Two tensor products on 𝒯 (lines 313, 471, 870-887)

Lines 313 and 471 define the tensor product of two states on one branchlike hypersurface (X₁ ⊗ X₂ "acting on the branchial graph consisting initially of states X₁ and X₂"). Lines 870-887 obtain ⊗ for hypergraph rewriting from the parallelism theorem: two sequentially independent productions applied to the same hypergraph G give a parallel production p₁ + p₂ : G ⇒ G′. The first is a product of two states, the second a parallel rewrite of one state. READ: G8 uses one symbol for both.

### C-8 Branchial graph and foliation (lines 303-313)

The time function satisfies "Δt ≠ 0 everywhere" (line 306), and the branchial graph joins two states "if and only if they share a common ancestor state Z" (line 313). With one initial state every pair of states shares it as an ancestor (as G7 line 269, `2111.03460_close_reading.md` C-3). G5 joins states that share an immediate predecessor. HGEvolve's default joins two states produced from the same input state by events that consume a common hyperedge (`efficient_rewriting_final/docs/en/ReferencePages/Symbols/HGEvolve.md:86`). READ.

### C-9 The hypergraph example is "largely irreducible but not entirely so" and more computationally than multicomputationally reducible (line 657)

> "illustrating that the composition ∘ and tensor product ⊗ operations are not purely additive (although they are both somewhat close), thus indicating that the system is largely (multi)computationally irreducible, but not entirely so ... we see that the hypergraph rewriting system is more computationally reducible than multicomputationally so (i.e. the composition operation ∘ is distorted more by the map Z′ than the tensor product operation ⊗)"

CONTRADICTED for ∘. MEASURED (`irreducibility_additivity.py`): for {{x,y},{x,z}} → {{x,z},{x,w},{y,w},{z,w}} from {{0,0},{0,0}}, with isomorphic states identified, every event joins a state with e edges to a state with e + 2 edges (434 of 434 events at 3 steps, 9938 of 9938 at 4 steps). Every path from X to Y therefore has length (|E(Y)| − |E(X)|)/2, and the least path length is additive under composition on all 210 reachable triples X → Y → Z at 3 steps and all 2895 at 4 steps. The time complexity of a morphism, as the number of rewrites it needs (lines 114, 471), composes additively, so Z′ does not distort ∘ on this example.

### C-10 Hypergraph state equivalence (lines 573, 577)

- Line 573: "we shall be using a generalized version of the 'uniqueness tree' isomorphism algorithm presented in [gorard6]" (Gorard 2016). CITED. Whether the algorithm decides isomorphism is OPEN in this repository (O8).
- Line 577: hypergraphs are a "finite collection/multiset of ordered relations" with E ⊆ P(V) \ {∅}. P(V) contains unordered sets without repetition, and a subset of it is not a multiset. READ (the definition of G1 Def 1, G5 Def 9 and G7, `2111.03460_close_reading.md` C-1).

### C-11 Adhesivity of the category of hypergraphs (line 868)

The category of hypergraphs and inclusions "is not strictly an adhesive category" and embeds in an adhesive category so that double-pushout rewriting can be performed (selective or partial adhesivity). CITED (Kissinger [kissinger]-[kissinger3]).

### C-12 Adjunction between irreducible computation and quantum time evolution (lines 937-987)

- Line 937: "In the most general case of a functorial quantum mechanics theory, defined by some functor from cobordisms to computations Z : ℬ → 𝒯, although it is not necessarily the case that Z will always be a strict inverse of Z′ ..., the two functors will at least be adjoint to one another." STATED.
- Line 963: "for any functor Z′ : 𝒯 → ℬ describing an irreducible computation, we can uniquely construct a corresponding functor Z : ℬ → 𝒯 describing a local quantum time evolution", and conversely (line 969). The displayed conditions (lines 966-973) are the naturality squares of ε and η. Lines 937-960 define the adjunction by universal arrows (displays at 940 and 953), which needs no triangle identities. Neither universal property is shown for any Z and Z′, and neither construction nor uniqueness is given. In §4 the quantum time evolution functor is Z : Bord₁^Riem → Vect (line 915), whose codomain is Vect, not 𝒯, and line 994 replaces 𝒯 by "a category of vector spaces and linear isomorphisms" for "the example considered initially". PROVED IN PAPER ("in this rather precise sense", line 963), proof fails: no construction, no universal property shown, and the functor of quantum mechanics does not have the codomain 𝒯.
- For the singleway case a right adjoint of Z′ exists without reference to quantum mechanics: when Z′ embeds a finite chain {0, ..., n} of configurations into (ℕ, ≤), the map m ↦ min(m, n) is its right adjoint, since Z′(c) ≤ m exactly when c ≤ min(m, n). This adjunction concerns posets and carries no vector-space structure.

### C-13 Multicomputational irreducibility is dual to the Atiyah-Segal sewing laws (lines 989-1009; abstract line 26)

"Hence, just as computational irreducibility may be said to be formally dual/adjoint to locality of time evolution in quantum mechanics, multicomputational irreducibility may be said to be formally dual/adjoint to adherence to the Atiyah-Segal sewing laws" (line 1009). The abstract says "outline how ... may be considered to be adjoint". STATED.

### C-14 Dual hypergraphs give a compact structure (lines 1118-1128)

"For the category of hypergraphs and subhypergraph inclusion maps, every hypergraph has a dual (that is trivially compatible with the dagger structure) given by the interchange of its vertex set V and its hyperedge set E". A compact structure requires unit and counit morphisms satisfying the snake equations. G8 gives the dual object only, no unit, counit or dagger on inclusion maps (an inclusion has no reverse inclusion unless it is an identity). STATED.

### C-15 Two notions of entropy (line 1166)

The thermodynamic entropy as a count of microstates and as a count of histories are "provably equivalent" in classical mechanics "due to Liouville's theorem", and "for more general multiway systems ... the two definitions of entropy diverge". STATED.

### C-16 Infinite-dimensional duals (line 1108)

"the axiom of choice here implies that the dual space is always of strictly larger dimension than the original space" for infinite-dimensional vector spaces. CITED (no reference given in the sentence), correct (Erdős-Kaplansky theorem).
