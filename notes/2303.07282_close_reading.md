## 2. Claims about hypergraph rewriting, causal graphs, the Raychaudhuri equation and singularity theorems

### C-1 Main result stated in the abstract (lines 67-75)

> "... we are able to show, through a combination of rigorous mathematical analysis and explicit numerical simulation, that the resulting discrete spacetimes converge asymptotically to either non-rotating Schwarzschild black hole solutions or maximally-rotating (extremal) Kerr black hole solutions, respectively."

- Argument: continuum analysis (WKB and the Lemaître-Tolman-Bondi metric in §3, the Ernst equation in §4), numerical solution with the Gravitas CCZ4 code (Tables 1-2), and runs of a rewriting rule on hypergraphs sprinkled into the analytic background (Tables 3-4, §5).
- G4 does not define convergence of a sequence of hypergraphs to a Lorentzian metric. The convergence statements in the paper are (i) late-time convergence of continuum solutions of the Einstein-Klein-Gordon system to Schwarzschild or Kerr, and (ii) mesh-refinement convergence of the Hamiltonian constraint in L1, L2 and L∞ norms (Table 1, line 3009; Table 2, line 4414; Table 3, line 5137; Table 4, line 5154). See also §3(h).

### C-2 Abstract on the discrete Raychaudhuri equation and trapped surfaces (lines 37-49)

> "Some, such as the discrete analog of Raychaudhuri's equation for the volumes of geodesic congruences, are subtle to formulate due to intrinsic differences in the behavior of discrete vs. continuous geodesics; for others, such as the definition of a trapped null surface, no appropriate translation is known for the general discrete case due to a lack of a priori coordinate information. It is therefore a non-trivial question to ask whether (and to what extent) there exist equivalently general conditions under which one expects discrete spacetimes to become geodesically incomplete ..."

G4 formulates no discrete Raychaudhuri equation.

### C-3 Concluding remarks (lines 5345-5395)

Lines 5347-5367 state that the article "has succeeded in demonstrating, through a combination of rigorous mathematical analysis and" numerical simulation, the evolution of a non-vacuum Wolfram model system and "the first robust analysis of gravitational collapse and singularity-formation behavior within a reasonably generic discrete spacetime setting". Lines 5370-5381 continue:

> "However, the techniques employed here are clearly exceedingly limited in scope, depending as they do upon an unphysically high degree of a priori symmetry, as well as an extremely idealized matter model ... it might conceivably be possible to formulate a more global topological theorem regarding geodesic incompleteness in generic Wolfram model systems, in a similar style to Penrose's original 1964 singularity theorem."

The abstract (lines 76-79) states that the results "offer hope that a more general, perhaps ultimately 'Penrose-like', singularity theorem may be provable in the discrete spacetime case too". G4 states no discrete singularity theorem. The title (lines 1-3) names "Discrete Singularity Theorems".

### C-4 Geodesic incompleteness in a causal graph (lines 325-335)

> "Penrose's notion of (null) geodesic incompleteness may be imported more-or-less 'wholesale': spacetime geodesics become directed paths in the causal graph ... the question of future/past geodesic incompleteness is equivalent to the question of whether there exist vertices within some subgraph of the causal graph (corresponding to the geodesic congruence) whose out/in-components are empty."

- Argument: a definition stated by analogy. G4 identifies geodesics with directed paths and a congruence with a subgraph, without an affine parameter, tangent direction, initial conditions or tidal tensor.
- In a finite causal graph every maximal directed path ends at a vertex with empty out-component. Under this definition every finite evolution is future incomplete. Lines 262-266 state that "if the number of gravitational degrees of freedom is necessarily finite, then all components and projections of discrete metric and curvature tensors will also be finite".

### C-5 Trapped surfaces (lines 357-363)

> "Finally, the lack of any a priori inner product structure (although such structures can be defined, albeit not uniquely) on causal graphs makes Penrose's original definition of trapped null surfaces hard to utilize directly, though equivalent descriptions in terms of cross-sectional areas of null congruences may be more amenable to immediate discretization."

G4 gives no definition of a discrete trapped surface or of the cross-sectional area of a congruence.

### C-6 Inner product on the hypergraph for finite differences (lines 1129-1135)

> "By equipping the hypergraph with a local inner product structure using discrete projections, as shown in Figure 2 (with the axioms of linearity and conjugate symmetry being satisfied whenever the hypergraph converges to a Riemannian manifold in the continuum limit), one can therefore define a local set of orthonormal coordinate axes on the hypergraph, over which a typical finite-difference scheme can be formulated."

G4 states the inner product axioms under the condition that the hypergraph "converges to a Riemannian manifold in the continuum limit" and does not define this convergence. The Figure 2 caption (lines 1195-1198) computes an inner product of 3/4 (TeX source `tex_2303/GravitationalCollapsePaper.tex:350`) on a hypergraph generated by the rule {{x,x,y},{x,z,w}} → {{w,w,v},{v,w,y},{z,y,v}}. G4 gives no proof that the projection is linear or symmetric on this hypergraph.

### C-7 Satisfaction of the Einstein equations in the continuum limit (§5, lines 4711-4739)

> "the condition of causal invariance allows one to define a compatible notion of discrete intrinsic curvature (based on the Ollivier-Ricci curvature construction ...) on both causal graphs and hypergraphs, and hence to construct a discrete analog of the Einstein-Hilbert action by taking the sum of the discrete Ricci scalar over the entire causal graph; if one further assumes that the hypergraph rewriting dynamics are weakly ergodic, such that, in particular, the net flux of causal edges through any given hypersurface in the causal graph eventually converges to zero, then one is analytically justified in exchanging this discrete sum for an integral in the continuum limit; finally, if one assumes that the hypergraph rewriting rule is asymptotically dimension preserving ... then it follows that the discrete Einstein-Hilbert action must be extremized in this limit. Thus, any Wolfram model rule which is causal invariant, weakly ergodic and asymptotically dimension preserving will be consistent with the Einstein field equations in the continuum limit."

- Argument: a summary of the derivation of arXiv:2004.14810 (G1), cited as [20][23][24] (line 4711).
- The summary uses three hypotheses:
  1. Causal invariance, defined in G1 Def 9 as eventual isomorphism of causal graphs without a stated sense of "eventually" ([`2004.14810_close_reading.md`](2004.14810_close_reading.md) §2.2).
  2. Weak ergodicity, described here as convergence to zero of "the net flux of causal edges through any given hypersurface". G4 does not define the flux, the hypersurface or the mode of convergence.
  3. Asymptotic dimension preservation, described as convergence of "the dimension of the limiting hypergraph" to a finite value.
- G4 adds no argument to G1 §3.3 for these steps. Lines 4736-4739 state that isomorphisms between causal graphs and hypergraphs are computed with the "uniqueness trees" algorithm [88] (Gorard 2016, line 5901).

### C-8 Rewriting rules and the update used (lines 4612-4652)

- G4 defines a rule R: H₁ = (V₁, E₁) → H₂ = (V₂, E₂) (eq. 296) and cites double-pushout rewriting over selective adhesive categories as making the semantics "fully rigorous" (line 4638).
- Lines 4640-4648 describe the update: the rule is applied "to every possible/matching (non-overlapping) subhypergraph at each step", and "there will, in general, exist multiple such choices of maximal non-overlapping sets of subhypergraphs". This is a maximal set of edge-disjoint matches per step. The repository's `MultiwayExec.singleWayStep` applies a maximal set of edge-disjoint matches, chosen greedily in enumeration order ([`singleway_vs_multiway.md`](singleway_vs_multiway.md)).
- The rule of all §5 runs is {{x,y},{y,z},{z,w},{w,v}} → {{y,u},{u,v},{w,x},{x,u}}, named in the captions of Tables 3-4 (lines 5140, 5157) and Figures 45-61 (lines 5163-5338). G4 gives no proof that this rule is causal invariant, weakly ergodic or asymptotically dimension preserving, the three hypotheses of C-7.

### C-9 Causal edge condition (lines 4670-4683)

> "the directed edge A → B exists if and only if In (B) ∩ Out (A) ≠ ∅, i.e. if and only if the input for rewrite event B makes use of hyperedges that were produced as part of the output of rewrite event A"

This names the hyperedges consumed and produced, which G1 Def 4 does not. G4 does not state whether In and Out are sets of hyperedge occurrences or of hyperedge values. The two readings differ when a state holds two equal hyperedges. The repository's causal graph on hyperedge identities is defined in `lean/InfraFormalization/CausalHistory.lean`.

### C-10 Massive scalar propagator on a causal graph (lines 4886-5048)

- G4 builds discrete massive propagators with Johnston's "hops and stops" formalism (line 4886, eq. 312 at line 4944) and states, citing Johnston [31][33] (2008, 2009), that they converge to the continuum Green's functions as the sprinkling density ρc → ∞ (eq. 317, lines 5001-5011).
- Lines 5015-5019 state that this analysis is "presented ... for the case of flat/Minkowski space". For curved spacetimes G4 uses the Klein-Gordon operator □ + m² + ζR(x) (eq. 318, line 5028) and states (lines 5044-5048): "By replacing the continuum Ricci scalar R(x) with the discrete (Ollivier-Ricci) scalar curvature for Wolfram model hypergraphs/causal graphs, we obtain a corresponding construction for arbitrary Wolfram model systems. Full details may be found in [28]." G4 gives no proof that this replacement gives the curved-space Green's function in the limit. Reference [28] is Gorard and Dannemann-Freitag (2023) (line 5571).

### C-11 Numerical results (Tables 1-4)

- Tables 1-2 (lines 3009, 4414): Hamiltonian-constraint convergence of the CCZ4 solver, approximately fourth order.
- Tables 3-4 (lines 5137, 5154): the same norms for evolution under the rule of C-8, captioned "approximately second-order convergence".
- The initial hypergraphs of the §5 runs are built "via Poisson sprinkling (with fixed sprinkling density ρc)" into the analytic Lemaître-Tolman-Bondi background (eq. 321, lines 5050-5068) and into the Weyl-Lewis-Papapetrou background (lines 5086-5095). The initial data come from the continuum solution. G4 does not state the update order, the number of events per unit of t, or the procedure that computes the Hamiltonian constraint from a hypergraph.

### C-12 Collapse condition (lines 444-457)

> "we rigorously derive a sufficient condition for the spherically-symmetric massive scalar field 'bubble collapse' problem to yield the idealized stellar collapse solution of Oppenheimer and Snyder."

- The condition is the inequality µM > (3/2)√(1 − 2M/λ) (eq. 183, line 2859).
- Argument: continuum analysis with WKB and the Lemaître-Tolman-Bondi metric, following Gonçalves and Moss [46] (line 5667).
- The derivation is in continuum general relativity and uses no discrete object.

### C-13 "This completes the proof" (line 4331, §4)

This line closes the argument that the Ernst potential of a uniformly rotating dust disk reduces to that of extremal Kerr as µ → µ₀ ≈ 4.63 (eq. 291, line 4328; line 4202). The argument follows Neugebauer and Meinel [47]-[49] and is in continuum general relativity. The discrete model enters only through the numerical runs.

## 3. Objects searched for

### (a) Discrete analog of the Raychaudhuri equation

Not present. G4 states that it is "subtle to formulate" (line 39), that discrete geodesic "collisions" are "far more common ... for the simple reason that there are fewer degrees of freedom" (lines 339-343), that discrete translations of the Penrose apparatus "remain elusive, or at the very least obscure" (lines 335-339), and that the discrete Raychaudhuri equation is "somewhat non-obvious" (line 351). G4 writes no evolution equation for a discrete expansion.

### (b) Expansion scalar θ on a hypergraph

Not defined. The word "expansion" in G4 refers to asymptotic series expansions in the WKB analysis (lines 2024-2555) and to expanding universes (lines 180-181).

### (c) Shear σ and vorticity ω

The words "shear" and "vorticity" do not occur in `papers/gorard_2303.07282.txt`.

### (d) Geodesic congruence on a hypergraph

Stated at line 335 as "some subgraph of the causal graph". G4 gives no tangent directions, affine parameter or initial conditions for it.

### (e) Discrete null, strong and dominant energy conditions

Not defined. The energy conditions in G4 are the continuum conditions of §1 (lines 167-183). The stress-energy tensor in the runs is the continuum Klein-Gordon tensor (eqs. 46-47, lines 1059-1066; eqs. 98-101, lines 1916-1939). Lines 355-357 state that "there has as of yet been no systematic investigation of discrete analogs of standard general relativistic energy conditions", and lines 5389-5391 name "analogs of relativistic energy conditions and equations of state within arbitrary Wolfram model causal graphs" as a direction for further investigation.

### (f) Discrete stress-energy tensor

Not defined. The stress-energy tensor of §2-§5 is the continuum Klein-Gordon tensor of the analytic background into which the hypergraph is sprinkled. The propagator of C-10 is a two-point function and not a local tensor.

### (g) Statement of a discrete singularity theorem

None. The title names "Discrete Singularity Theorems". The paper states a singularity theorem as a possible later result (lines 76-79, 5377-5381).

### (h) Mode of convergence to Schwarzschild and Kerr

G4 uses two senses of convergence:

1. Late-time convergence of continuum solutions (lines 2667-2673 for the Lemaître-Tolman-Bondi exterior; lines 3168-3190 and §4 for the Weyl-Lewis-Papapetrou disk). These follow Gonçalves and Moss [46] and Neugebauer and Meinel [47]-[49].
2. Mesh-refinement convergence of the Hamiltonian constraint in L1, L2 and L∞ (Tables 1-4). This concerns the CCZ4 scheme (Tables 1-2) and the rewriting runs on sprinkled initial data (Tables 3-4).

G4 defines no convergence of hypergraphs to a Lorentzian manifold (pointwise on metric components, Γ-convergence, Gromov-Hausdorff, spectral or other).

## 5. Summary

- The title names non-vacuum solutions, gravitational collapse and discrete singularity theorems.
- Non-vacuum solutions and collapse are treated in continuum general relativity: a massive Klein-Gordon field in spherical or axial symmetry, in the WKB regime, reduces to Lemaître-Tolman-Bondi dust collapsing to Schwarzschild, and to a Weyl-Lewis-Papapetrou dust disk whose µ → µ₀ limit is extremal Kerr, following Gonçalves-Moss and Neugebauer-Meinel. The CCZ4 code reports fourth-order convergence for these problems (Tables 1-2).
- G4 contains no discrete Raychaudhuri equation, discrete expansion scalar, discrete energy condition, discrete trapped surface or discrete singularity theorem. Lines 76-79 and 5377-5381 state a discrete Penrose-type theorem as a possible later result.
- The Wolfram model part (§5) uses the hypotheses of causal invariance, weak ergodicity and asymptotic dimension preservation from G1, and replaces the continuum Ricci scalar by the Ollivier-Ricci scalar in the curved-space Klein-Gordon operator without a convergence proof (lines 5044-5048).

## 6. Definitions a Lean formalisation of G4's claims would need

All items are OPEN.

1. A notion of convergence of hypergraphs to a Lorentzian manifold (M, g), for example Gromov-Hausdorff convergence of spatial hypergraphs, measured Gromov-Hausdorff convergence with the sprinkling measure, or spectral convergence of discrete d'Alembertians.
2. A definition of causal invariance with an explicit quantifier, for example ∃N ∀n ≥ N, all causal graphs truncated at n events are isomorphic.
3. A notion of dimension of a hypergraph, and a proof that a given rule is asymptotically dimension preserving.
4. A discrete geodesic congruence with (a) a direction at each vertex along the congruence, (b) a notion of neighbouring geodesics, (c) a cross-sectional area. A discrete expansion, shear and vorticity, and a discrete Raychaudhuri equation, need these.
5. A discrete stress-energy tensor on hyperedges or causal edges, with discrete null, strong and dominant energy conditions.
6. A discrete trapped surface, for example through cross-sectional areas (line 363).
7. A statement of a discrete singularity theorem: hypotheses on the causal graph, a discrete energy condition and a discrete trapped surface imply a directed path in the causal graph that cannot be extended, under hypotheses on the rule that exclude incompleteness caused only by finiteness of the graph (C-4).

None of items 1-7 appears in G4. Items 1 and 4-7 do not appear in G1. Items 2 and 3 appear in G1 without the precision stated here.
