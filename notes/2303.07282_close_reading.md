## 2. Inventory of claims about hypergraph rewriting, causal graphs, discrete Raychaudhuri, discrete singularity theorems

### C-1 Main advertised result (Abstract, lines 69–75)
> "... we are able to show, through a combination of rigorous mathematical analysis and explicit numerical simulation, that the resulting discrete spacetimes converge asymptotically to either non-rotating Schwarzschild black hole solutions or maximally-rotating (extremal) Kerr black hole solutions, respectively."
- **Argument type:** mix of continuum-PDE analysis (WKB + Lemaître-Tolman-Bondi / Ernst equation) + numerical simulation via the Gravitas CCZ4 code + a further numerical run where hypergraphs are sprinkled into the same analytic background.
- **Gap:** the word "discrete spacetimes converge" does not refer to convergence of a hypergraph-defined object to a metric. It refers to (i) asymptotic late-time convergence of the *continuum* solution of Einstein + Klein-Gordon to Schwarzschild/Kerr, and (ii) numerical convergence of CCZ4 finite-difference data (Tables 1–4, lines 3002, 4407, 5130, 5147) with respect to L¹, L², L∞ norms. No mathematical statement "hypergraph H_n → Lorentzian manifold M" is proven or even precisely formulated. See also C-12.

### C-2 Abstract admission regarding discrete Raychaudhuri and discrete singularity theorems (lines 38–49)
> "Some, such as the discrete analog of Raychaudhuri's equation for the volumes of geodesic congruences, are subtle to formulate due to intrinsic differences in the behavior of discrete vs. continuous geodesics; for others, such as the definition of a trapped null surface, no appropriate translation is known for the general discrete case due to a lack of a priori coordinate information. It is therefore a non-trivial question to ask whether (and to what extent) there exist equivalently general conditions under which one expects discrete spacetimes to become geodesically incomplete ..."
- This is an explicit concession that **no discrete Raychaudhuri equation is formulated in this paper**.

### C-3 Closing concession (Section 6, lines 5369–5381)
> "However, the techniques employed here are clearly exceedingly limited in scope, depending as they do upon an unphysically high degree of a priori symmetry, as well as an extremely idealized matter model ... it might conceivably be possible to formulate a more global topological theorem regarding geodesic incompleteness in generic Wolfram model systems, in a similar style to Penrose's original 1964 singularity theorem."
- **Argument type:** pure statement of aspiration.
- **Gap:** no discrete singularity theorem appears anywhere in the body. The title of the paper is therefore *aspirational*, not descriptive.

### C-4 Discrete geodesic incompleteness by wholesale import (lines 323–335)
> "Penrose's notion of (null) geodesic incompleteness may be imported more-or-less 'wholesale': spacetime geodesics become directed paths in the causal graph ... future/past geodesic incompleteness is equivalent to the question of whether there exist vertices within some subgraph of the causal graph (corresponding to the geodesic congruence) whose out/in-components are empty."
- **Argument type:** definition-by-analogy.
- **Gap:** identifies "geodesic" with "directed path" and "congruence" with "subgraph" without justifying that these inherit any Raychaudhuri-style structure (affine parameter, tangent transport, parallel initial conditions, tidal tensor). Paths in a finite causal graph trivially terminate at sinks, so "geodesic incompleteness" is vacuous or trivial for finite systems (compare lines 262–268 where Gorard himself concedes that finite-DOF models have no curvature divergences).

### C-5 Trapped surface and cross-section (lines 357–363)
> "Finally, the lack of any a priori inner product structure (although such structures can be defined, albeit not uniquely) on causal graphs makes Penrose's original definition of trapped null surfaces hard to utilize directly, though equivalent descriptions in terms of cross-sectional areas of null congruences may be more amenable to immediate discretization."
- **Argument type:** acknowledged open problem, no construction given.
- **Gap:** no definition of a discrete trapped surface or cross-sectional area is supplied anywhere in the paper.

### C-6 Hypergraph "inner product" for finite differences (lines 1131–1135)
> "By equipping the hypergraph with a local inner product structure using discrete projections, as shown in Figure 2 (with the axioms of linearity and conjugate symmetry being satisfied whenever the hypergraph converges to a Riemannian manifold in the continuum limit), one can therefore define a local set of orthonormal coordinate axes on the hypergraph, over which a typical finite-difference scheme can be formulated."
- **Argument type:** declaration.
- **Gap:** "whenever the hypergraph converges to a Riemannian manifold in the continuum limit" is the entire load-bearing hypothesis of the construction, and the limit is neither defined nor shown to exist. Inner-product axioms are claimed to hold "whenever" this convergence happens; no proof, and no specification of the convergence mode. Figure 2 caption gives a concrete example ("dropping a perpendicular ... yielding a normalized inner product value of 4/3", line 1198) that depends on a 2-D manifold-like embedding which is itself never constructed from the hypergraph data.

### C-7 Provable satisfaction of EFE in the continuum limit (Section 5 recap, lines 4713–4734)
> "the condition of causal invariance allows one to define a compatible notion of discrete intrinsic curvature (based on the Ollivier-Ricci curvature construction) on both causal graphs and hypergraphs, and hence to construct a discrete analog of the Einstein-Hilbert action by taking the sum of the discrete Ricci scalar ... if one further assumes that the hypergraph rewriting dynamics are weakly ergodic, such that ... the net flux of causal edges through any given hypersurface in the causal graph eventually converges to zero, then one is analytically justified in exchanging this discrete sum for an integral in the continuum limit; finally, if one assumes that the hypergraph rewriting rule is asymptotically dimension preserving ... then it follows that the discrete Einstein-Hilbert action must be extremized in this limit. Thus, any Wolfram model rule which is causal invariant, weakly ergodic and asymptotically dimension preserving will be consistent with the Einstein field equations in the continuum limit."
- **Argument type:** recapitulation of the earlier derivation of arXiv:2004.14810.
- **Gap:** depends on three declared-but-undefined hypotheses:
  1. *causal invariance* — "eventual isomorphism of causal graphs"; undefined (cf. 2004.14810 close-reading, D-1).
  2. *weak ergodicity* — undefined flux measure, undefined hypersurface in a causal graph, undefined convergence.
  3. *asymptotically dimension preserving* — dimension defined by a ball-growth fit with no proven convergence.
  Section 5 does not add rigour to 2004.14810; it repeats the claim under the same unproven hypotheses.

### C-8 Hypergraph rewriting specification (lines 4625–4652)
Rule R: H₁=(V₁,E₁) → H₂=(V₂,E₂); DPO rewriting in selective adhesive categories is cited as "fully rigorous" (line 4638). Concrete rule used in all Section-5 numerical runs: `{{x, y}, {y, z}, {z, w}, {w, v}} → {{y, u}, {u, v}, {w, x}, {x, u}}` (e.g. line 5140).
- **Argument type:** standard construction by citation.
- **Gap:** no proof is offered that this specific rule is *causal invariant*, *weakly ergodic*, or *asymptotically dimension preserving* — that is, the three load-bearing conditions of C-7 are *assumed*, not *verified*, for the rule used in the numerics.

### C-9 Causal graph edge condition (lines 4672–4683)
> "the directed edge A → B exists if and only if In (B) ∩ Out (A) ≠ ∅, i.e. if and only if the input for rewrite event B makes use of hyperedges that were produced as part of the output of rewrite event A"
- This is the same quasi-formal definition as Def 4 of 2004.14810; inherits the same informality.

### C-10 Massive scalar Green's function for hypergraphs (lines 4886–5048)
Discrete massive propagator matrices (e.g. eq. 312, line 4944) are constructed by the Dowker-Johnston "hops and stops" formalism and claimed to converge to continuum Green's functions as sprinkling density ρ_c → ∞ (eq. 317, line 5009).
- **Argument type:** citation of prior results (Johnston 1988, Dowker et al.; see refs [31], [32], [33], [34]).
- **Gap:** the cited Johnston results are for Poisson sprinkling in Minkowski space. Gorard extends them to curved/Wolfram-model spacetimes by the substitution ` + m² + ζR(x)` (eq. 318, line 5026), replacing R by the "Ollivier-Ricci scalar curvature for Wolfram model hypergraphs/causal graphs" (line 5046). **No proof** that this substitution reproduces the curved-space Green's function in the continuum limit — it's stated at line 5044 as a direct "replacement." Referred back to ref [28] for details.

### C-11 Numerical results (Tables 1–4)
Hamiltonian-constraint L¹/L²/L∞ convergence rates under mesh refinement: Tables 1–2 (lines 3002, 4407) test the CCZ4 finite-difference solver (~O(4)); Tables 3–4 (lines 5130, 5147) test the "pure Wolfram model evolution" with rule `{{x,y},{y,z},{z,w},{w,v}} → {{y,u},{u,v},{w,x},{x,u}}` (~O(2)).
- **Argument type:** numerical demonstration.
- **Gap:** in Tables 3–4 the "pure Wolfram model" still uses Poisson sprinkling into an analytic Schwarzschild / Weyl-Lewis-Papapetrou background to produce the initial hypergraph (lines 5066–5091). Initial data are extracted from the exact continuum solution; the rewriting rule is then applied and the Hamiltonian constraint measured. This is not an ab initio Wolfram-model derivation of Schwarzschild/Kerr; it measures numerical consistency of a discretization scheme with analytic initial data, not convergence of a discrete object to a continuum manifold.

### C-12 "Rigorous derivation" of collapse condition (line 453)
> "we rigorously derive a sufficient condition for the spherically-symmetric massive scalar field 'bubble collapse' problem to yield the idealized stellar collapse solution of Oppenheimer and Snyder."
- Referent: the inequality `µM > (3/2)√(1 − 2M/λ)` (eq. 183, line 2858).
- **Argument type:** standard continuum-PDE analysis (WKB + LTB) following Gonçalves–Moss [46], correctly executed.
- **Gap:** the derivation is of continuum general relativity, with no discrete ingredient. The title of the paper names discrete singularity theorems, and this derivation contains no discrete ingredient.

### C-13 "This completes the proof" (line 4331, Section 4 Kerr analysis)
The closing line of the argument that the Ernst potential for a uniformly-rotating dust disk reduces to that of extremal Kerr in the limit µ → µ₀ ≈ 4.63 (eq. 291).
- **Argument type:** continuum computation following Neugebauer-Meinel.
- **Gap:** as with C-12, this is purely continuum GR. Discretization enters only through numerical simulation afterwards.

## 3. Objects specifically hunted for

### (a) Discrete analog of Raychaudhuri's equation
**Not present.** The only discussion is that it is "subtle to formulate" (line 39), that discrete geodesic collisions are "far more common ... for the simple reason that there are fewer degrees of freedom" (lines 340–347), and that appropriate discrete analogs "remain elusive, or at the very least obscure" (line 337). No equation for the time evolution of any discrete θ is written down.

### (b) Expansion scalar θ on a hypergraph
**Not defined.** The word "expansion" in the paper refers only to FRW scale factor (line 2454) or to the expansion of the universe. No discrete θ(λ) is constructed.

### (c) Shear σ, vorticity ω
**Not mentioned** anywhere in the paper. (Grep for "shear" / "vorticity" returns zero hits in the body.)

### (d) Geodesic congruence on a hypergraph
**Declared** (line 335) as "some subgraph of the causal graph" corresponding to a family of directed paths. Not constructed from any tangent-vector / affine-parameter data; no continuous family of paths with initial conditions is specified.

### (e) Discrete null / strong / dominant energy conditions
**Not defined.** All energy conditions in the paper are continuum (lines 167–183). In the numerical runs, the stress-energy tensor is the standard continuum T_µν of the massive Klein-Gordon field (eqs. 46–47, 98–101). No discrete T_µν construction and no discrete energy condition appears. The concluding remarks (line 5390) call for future work on "analogs of relativistic energy conditions and equations of state within arbitrary Wolfram model causal graphs" — i.e., confirming they are not done here.

### (f) Discrete stress-energy tensor
**Not defined.** The "stress-energy tensor" used throughout §2–§5 is the continuum Klein-Gordon T_µν of the analytic background into which the hypergraph is sprinkled. The closest discrete object is the matter Green's function of C-10, but that is a two-point function, not a local tensor.

### (g) Precise discrete singularity theorem statement
**None.** The paper's title promises "Discrete Singularity Theorems" (plural). No such theorem is stated, much less proved. The only statement is the concluding aspiration at lines 5377–5381.

### (h) Asymptotic convergence to Schwarzschild / Kerr — what mode of convergence?
Two disjoint senses are used interchangeably:
1. **Continuum late-time convergence** (lines 2667–2673, 3168–3190): the Lemaître-Tolman-Bondi / Weyl-Lewis-Papapetrou exterior solution reduces to Schwarzschild/extremal-Kerr in the appropriate limit. This is standard GR and correctly derived from cited references (Gonçalves-Moss [46]; Neugebauer-Meinel [47–49]).
2. **Numerical convergence** (Tables 1–4): mesh-refinement convergence of the Hamiltonian constraint in L¹, L², L∞. This tests that the CCZ4 finite-difference scheme (Tables 1–2) or the hypergraph-rewriting scheme applied to sprinkled initial data (Tables 3–4) is a stable approximation of the Einstein equations. It does **not** test convergence of a hypergraph to a manifold.
Neither mode addresses "hypergraph H_n → Lorentzian manifold (M, g_Schw)" in any topology. No pointwise-on-metric, Γ-limit, Gromov-Hausdorff, spectral, or PDE-solution convergence is defined.

## 5. Verdict (~200 words)

The title advertises three things: (a) non-vacuum solutions in the Wolfram model, (b) gravitational collapse, (c) discrete singularity theorems. Items (a) and (b) are executed as a standard-GR exercise: a massive Klein-Gordon field in spherical/axial symmetry, treated via WKB, reduces (continuum-analytically) to Lemaître-Tolman-Bondi dust collapsing to Schwarzschild and to a Weyl-Lewis-Papapetrou dust disk collapsing, in the µ → µ₀ limit, to extremal Kerr. Both reductions are correctly derived from the existing Gonçalves-Moss and Neugebauer-Meinel literature; the CCZ4 numerical code reproduces them to fourth order. Item (c) is **entirely absent**: no discrete Raychaudhuri equation, no discrete expansion scalar, no discrete energy condition, no discrete trapped surface, no discrete singularity theorem. The paper explicitly concedes (lines 5377–5381) that a discrete Penrose-style theorem remains future work. The Wolfram-model connection in §5 rests on the unproved hypotheses of causal invariance, weak ergodicity, and asymptotic dimension preservation — identical to the gaps flagged in 2004.14810 — plus the additional unproven substitution of the Ollivier-Ricci scalar into the curved-space Klein-Gordon operator. A referee demanding rigorous proofs would accept the continuum collapse computations, reject "discrete singularity theorems" as a promise not delivered, and send §5 back with the instruction to prove the three structural hypotheses first.

## 6. Formal program (what Lean would need)

To make even the modest claims of this paper formal, one would need, in order of priority:

1. **A formal notion of "hypergraph ⟶ (Lorentzian manifold, metric g)".** Without this, "in the continuum limit" is undefined. Candidates: Gromov-Hausdorff on the spatial hypergraph sequence; measured-GH with sprinkling measure; spectral convergence of discrete d'Alembertians.
2. **A formal definition of causal invariance.** The "eventually isomorphic" phrasing needs a quantifier: ∃N ∀n≥N ∀paths p₁,p₂ of length n, CausalGraph(p₁)|_n ≅ CausalGraph(p₂)|_n, or analogous.
3. **A formal notion of "dimension" of a hypergraph and a proof that specific rules are asymptotically dimension-preserving.** Ollivier-style curvatures and Knopf-style dimensions are candidate starting points.
4. **A discrete geodesic-congruence object** equipped with (a) a tangent-direction assignment at each vertex along the congruence, (b) a notion of neighboring geodesics, (c) an analog of cross-sectional area. Only then does a discrete θ, σ, ω make sense, and only then can a discrete Raychaudhuri equation be stated.
5. **A discrete stress-energy tensor** localized on hyperedges or causal-graph edges, together with discrete null / strong / dominant energy conditions.
6. **A discrete trapped surface definition** (cross-sectional-area version looks most tractable; line 363).
7. **A formal statement of a discrete singularity theorem**: hypotheses on the causal graph + discrete energy condition + discrete trapped surface ⟹ existence of a finite directed path in the causal graph that cannot be extended. Under which hypotheses on the rewrite rule does this become a *non-trivial* statement (i.e., an incompleteness not forced by the mere finiteness of the graph)?

None of 1–7 is in the paper. Items 1, 4, 5, 6, 7 are not in 2004.14810 either; items 2, 3 inherit the informality of 2004.14810 and remain open.
