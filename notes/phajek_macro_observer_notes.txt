Observations extracted from a PHAJEK note to SWOLF (macro-observer
perspective on the infrageometry framework). Recorded here for the
project record. Two themes: assigning Riemannian geometry to a graph,
and the feasibility of the discrete-to-continuous passage.

=== Theme 1: a graph maps to a MODULI of Riemannian manifolds ===

PHAJEK's framing: edge-length embeddings of a graph into R^n followed
by TDA / DEC / manifold-learning give candidate Riemannian geometries
as submanifolds of R^n. Both the embedding and the shape detection are
highly non-unique, so a single graph corresponds to a whole moduli of
Riemannian manifolds, not a single one.

Useful points for this project:

1. This is an independent articulation of our F1 refutation ("no
   canonical metric"). It sharpens it: the correct object is not "the
   manifold a graph converges to" but "the moduli of manifolds
   compatible with the graph". Our HypergraphMetricOriginObligation
   Props should be read as existential over that moduli, never as
   pinning a unique target. Worth stating explicitly in the obligation
   docs and in the K1 audit framing.

2. Nash-embedding observation (refutation-grade). PHAJEK notes that
   merely assuming "shortest-path distance = geodesic distance of the
   assigned Riemannian geometry" already lands you in the moduli,
   because every Riemannian manifold embeds isometrically in some R^n
   by Nash. Consequence: shortest-path distance alone can NEVER pin a
   unique manifold. This is a clean, citable strengthening of F1 and a
   candidate Lean-or-prose statement: "the graph-distance-as-geodesic
   assumption is satisfied by a positive-dimensional family of
   manifolds". Any derivation that treats the limit manifold as unique
   without further structure is therefore underdetermined.

3. Discrete invariants act as obstructions that cut down the moduli:
     - clique size  -> lower bound on embedding dimension
     - discrete curvature -> curvature constraints
     - clique complex -> topology
   This is exactly what our RuleSignatureScreener computes (Forman /
   Ollivier curvature, edge / vertex counts, angular profile). The
   conceptual reframe worth adopting: the screener is an
   obstruction calculator that narrows the moduli. A rule "passes"
   when its screened invariants are consistent with the target moduli
   class; it can never single out one manifold, only a class.

4. Gromov coarse-graining is the positive route. Large-scale / coarse
   invariants can collapse the moduli to finitely many classes, or a
   unique class, depending on observer scale and graph properties.
   This is precisely the pointed-Gromov-Hausdorff and
   metric-measure-GH infrastructure already on the task list (the
   project-own pointed-GH task). That task should be understood as the
   moduli-collapsing machinery, not just a distance definition.

5. Proposed precise problem: formulate the map from equivalence
   classes of graphs to equivalence classes of Riemannian manifolds,
   survey existing methods, position infrageometry. PHAJEK frames this
   as a theoretical LaTeX document. It overlaps our K3 (infrageometry
   continuum-limits paper) and K5 (Wolfram-to-GR bridge paper).

=== Theme 2: a measure-theoretic obstruction to discrete-to-continuous ===

PHAJEK reports that HVLE has statistical counterarguments against the
discrete-to-continuous direction, framed via a projective limit and
the distinction between probability distributions and charges (signed
or merely finitely-additive measures). Keywords given: Orbanz
framework, Suslin-type issues.

Useful points for this project:

6. This is a SECOND, independent obstruction class, distinct from the
   F1-F7 structural refutations. It is measure-theoretic. A continuum
   limit of a sequence of finite rewriting states is a projective
   limit; the obstruction is that a projective family of
   finite-dimensional laws need not extend to a genuine countably-
   additive probability measure. It can extend only to a charge, or
   run into non-measurable (Suslin-type) pathologies. The Kolmogorov
   extension theorem needs topological regularity that is not
   automatic here.

7. Relation to our existing audit. Our F5 ("ill-defined limit") is in
   the same spirit but vaguer. The HVLE / Orbanz framing is sharper
   because it names the precise failure mode: the projective limit is
   a charge, not a probability measure. F5 should be annotated with
   this sharper statement.

8. Relation to our convergence work. Our weak-convergence results
   (counting measure to Lebesgue on the interval, the Wasserstein-rate
   results) are MARGINAL statements and remain safe. The obstruction
   bites at the level of the JOINT law of the whole limiting object,
   not the one-dimensional marginals. Worth recording explicitly so
   the scope of the A-series convergence results is not overstated:
   they establish marginal weak convergence, not the existence of a
   genuine limiting process.

9. Proposed work: place hypergraph rewriting explicitly in the
   projective-limit setting, identify when the statistical obstruction
   applies, and explore renormalization / controlled coarse-graining /
   variational principles as ways around it. PHAJEK frames this as a
   second theoretical LaTeX document. It overlaps our K8 (discrete
   d'Alembertian / BD-flag) and K5, and is the natural home for a
   precise statement of F5.
