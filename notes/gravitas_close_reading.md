## 1. What GII and GH solve

GII solves the ADM equations by finite differences, with fourth-order Runge-Kutta time integration. GII line 1168: "Gravitas uses a standard fourth-order explicit Runge-Kutta method ... making use of the fourth-order centered finite-difference stencils proposed by Zlochower, Baker, Campanelli and Lousto ... or fourth-order upwind finite-difference stencils in the presence of non-zero advection terms", with "a Kreiss-Oliger dissipation term". Lines 1226-1277 give the vacuum ADM evolution equation, the 1 + log slicing condition and the minimal distortion shift condition for the Brill-Lindquist collision. GII line 1337 states that BSSN and CCZ4 "have previously been implemented in an experimental form within" G3, "although this experimental implementation has not yet been fully integrated with the design of the rest of the Gravitas framework".

In GII, hypergraph rewriting is the refinement and coarsening step of the finite-difference mesh. GII line 1168: the algorithms are defined over hypergraphs "which in turn automatically coarsen and refine via hypergraph rewriting in order to adapt to the topology and dynamics of the problem being solved, using a hypergraph generalization of the local adaptive mesh refinement algorithm developed by Gropp, Berger, Oliger and Colella", with "a binary function which determines whether a given vertex or subhypergraph should be tagged for refinement". The abstract (line 24) names "adaptive refinement algorithms based on hypergraph rewriting via Wolfram model evolution". Line 1168 refers the details to G3 (`gorard9`, line 1424). The rewriting rules of this refinement are given in G3 lines 2226-2234 and G3 Figure 7: an edge {u, v} with a tagged endpoint is subdivided into {u, w}, {w, v}, and a tagged vertex w on {u, w}, {w, v} is removed by joining {u, v}. The tagging function is G3 eq. 152, a threshold on the change of a scalar φ across a vertex (φ = ψ in G3 §5). G4 Figure 3 (G4 line 1405) repeats the two rules. GII and GH name no other rewriting rule. The words "substitution", "sprinkling", "Poisson" and "cut-off" do not occur in GII or GH. "Causal" occurs in GII only in bibliography titles, and in GH in bibliography titles and once in the body (line 33, "causal dynamical triangulations"). The rule {{x,y},{y,z},{z,w},{w,v}} -> {{y,u},{u,v},{w,x},{x,u}} occurs in none of GI, GII and GH.

GH solves the Valencia equations of general relativistic hydrodynamics coupled to the ADM equations, by finite volumes on a hypergraph mesh. GH line 369 subdivides the spacetime into "simply-connected n-dimensional submanifolds Ω ⊆ M (known as "control volumes", "computational cells", or "nodes" within Gravitas's terminology)". Line 390: the "weak" integral form "can then be solved directly using Gravitas's hypergraph-based finite-volume numerical algorithms". Line 525: "We run all simulations with a hypergraph resolution of 10,000 vertices", with "an adaptive fourth-order Runge-Kutta algorithm" and an "adaptive hypergraph refinement algorithm (applied here as a preconditioning step for the initial data)". Line 364: the elliptic constraints are solved "typically by means of an iterative solver". Line 499: conservative-to-primitive inversion by Newton-Raphson. The only rewriting named in GH is the refinement step, applied to the initial data.

The equations of GH are continuum equations on a smooth manifold. GH line 42: "Assuming a spacetime given by a smooth n-dimensional Lorentzian manifold (M, g)". Line 246 rewrites the source terms "purely in terms of" T^μν, the primitive variables, α, β^μ, γ_μν and K_μν, so that fluid and metric evolve together (line 241). No term of the evolution or constraint equations of GH (lines 260-364) contains a discretisation scale or a quantity of the hypergraph.

"Discretization scale" in GII and GH is a parameter of the mesh construction. GII line 1180: "a discretization scale (which determines the hypergraph density, as described in [G2] and [G3]) of 1.2" with 400 vertices. GII line 1277: "a resolution of 10,000 vertices and a discretization scale of 0.2M" for the binary. GH line 741: the accretion rates are "slightly lower than in the analytic solution of Michel, with this discrepancy vanishing in the limit as the discretization scale goes to zero". Line 864 states a discrepancy against Petrich, Shapiro and Teukolsky that vanishes "in the limit as the discretization scale and the black hole spin both go to zero". Line 1044: the rates "decrease monotonically as the discretization scale of the underlying spacetime increases", for J = 0.99M about six times as rapidly with Γ = 2 and about two times as rapidly with Γ = 4/3 as for a non-rotating black hole, and the drag instability "may be a purely numerical artefact". GH gives no value of the discretization scale and no table or figure of an accretion rate or a drag force against it. The figure files of GH (TeX lines 529-933) are hypergraph drawings. In GC the discretization scale is the Euclidean link radius of the sampled points (`gravitas_code_audit.md` §1). An accretion rate that converges to the analytic solution as the scale goes to zero is the behaviour of a discretisation error of the mesh.

GI states that the rewriting and canonicalization algorithms of Gravitas were applied to binary black hole collisions in G3. GI line 35: "the abstract hypergraph rewriting ... and hypergraph canonicalization ... algorithms that underlie its numerical subsystems, have already been applied to great effect in the study of binary black hole collisions" (citation key `gorard8`, G3, GI line 1582). GI line 1459 announces mergers and wave extraction for GII.

The rΨ4 curve of GII Figure 54 agrees with the 800-vertex CCZ4 curve of G3 Figure 39 to about two pixels (amplitude ratio 0.9996, time offset 0.004 M) and carries the same 0.1-0.3 M wiggles (MEASURED, [`results/phase2_continuum/rpsi4_digitisation.md`](../results/phase2_continuum/rpsi4_digitisation.md)). GII lines 1226-1277 and 1321-1328 state a different formulation (ADM), resolution (10,000 vertices) and system (masses M at separation 2M) from G3 Figure 39 (CCZ4, 200-800 vertices, masses 0.5M at separation 1M, G3 lines 4391-4440).

## 2. The pure Wolfram model runs of G3 in these sources

G3 shares no file with GII or GH.

## 4. Other outputs of the Wolfram Institute on these claims

The Wolfram Institute output list tagged with the author's name (wolframinstitute.org/output/tag/Jonathan+Gorard, retrieved 2026-10-24) has 16 entries. The arXiv identifier and the DOI below are not on the list. They come from G4 reference [28] and from the entry page of the cosine-rule paper. The entries: G1, G2, G3, G4, GI, GII, GH, "Some Quantum Mechanical Properties of the Wolfram Model", the ZX-calculus papers I and II, "Fast Automated Reasoning over String Diagrams using Multiway Causal Structure", "A Functorial Perspective on (Multi)computational Irreducibility", "Homotopies in Multiway (Non-Deterministic) Rewriting Systems as n-Fold Categories", "Pregeometric Spaces from Wolfram Model Rewriting Systems as Homotopy Types", "Axiomatic Quantum Field Theory in Discrete Spacetime via Multiway Causal Structure: The Case of Entanglement Entropies" (arXiv:2301.12455) and "A Cosine Rule-Based Discrete Sectional Curvature for Graphs" (Journal of Complex Networks, doi 10.1093/comnet/cnad022). Two of them bear on the black-hole and general-relativity claims:

- arXiv:2301.12455 is reference [28] of G4, the source of the scalar field on causal graphs and of the replacement of R(x) by the Ollivier-Ricci scalar (G4 lines 4743-4747, 5044-5048).
- The cosine-rule paper builds random geometric graphs by sprinkling on manifolds of constant curvature, defines a metric distortion of the graph metric against the manifold metric, and shows how sprinkling algorithms can be refined to produce "hard annulus random geometric graphs with minimal metric distortion". Its abstract states that the error of the estimated curvature diminishes as the mean metric distortion goes to zero. The reproduction's dimension and curvature readings on plain cut-off clouds are UNCALIBRATED ([`empirical_refutation.md`](empirical_refutation.md) section 6).

The other entries concern quantum mechanics, category theory and complexity, and state no results about black holes or the Einstein equations.

## 5. Figure style of the black-hole figures

From the figure files, with `scripts/python/dominant_colours.py` (output `results/phase2_continuum/dominant_colours.out`):

- Hypergraphs are `Graph` objects coloured by one scalar with `ColorData["LightTemperatureMap"]` (`DiscreteHypersurfaceDecomposition.wl:96-98`): dark blue (low) through pale cyan and pale yellow to orange-red (high). The dominant colours of the G3 §6 panel `NumericalRelativity167.png` are pale cyan (208, 240, 240), light blue (144, 208, 240) and pale yellow (240, 240, 208), and those of the GII binary `GravitasFigure102.png` are the same cyan and blue.
- Layouts. The GII captions (lines 1189-1222) name two: "with spatial coordinate information assigned to the vertices" and "without any vertex coordinate information assigned". GC has two: a three-dimensional layout with the scalar as the third coordinate, (x, y, −K) (`GravitasFigure93.png`), and the default `Graph` layout, which for the binary shows two holes that join into one (`GravitasFigure102.png`, G3 `NumericalRelativity167.png` to `NumericalRelativity170.png`). GH (lines 525 and 673) also places vertices at their two-dimensional spatial coordinates (`papers/tex_2402/AccretionFigure13.png`) and uses the three-dimensional layout (`AccretionFigure21.png`).
- Edges are thin and semi-transparent. Vertices are small discs with a dark outline, coloured in GII, white-filled in G3 §6, and omitted at 10,000 vertices in GH. No hypergraph drawing has axes, a frame or a colour bar. GII figures are notebook screenshots that keep the In[ ]:= input and the Out[ ]= label, and the binary evolution is one output list of seven graphs (`GravitasFigure102.png`).
- Causal graphs (G3 Figures 33 and 76, `NumericalRelativity88.png`, `NumericalRelativity171.png`) are layered digraphs with orange event vertices (mean colour 247, 163, 1) and dark red arrows (176, 80, 80 and 144, 16, 16), without labels.
- rΨ4 plots (G3 Figures 39, 49, 78, 84 and GII Figure 54) are default `ListLinePlot` output: axes crossing at the origin, labels "Time (M)" and "rψ4", t from 1 to 12, the default colours blue, orange and green for 200, 400 and 800 vertices, and a legend "Low Resolution (200 Vertices)", "Medium Resolution (400 Vertices)", "High Resolution (800 Vertices)". GII Figure 54 has one blue curve and no legend.
